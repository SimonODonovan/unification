⚠️  The 07:05 flight on Saturday governs this whole leg

Everything below is built backwards from a 07:05 departure from Edinburgh Airport on Saturday 17 October. That means being at the terminal by about 05:15, which in turn means either a 04:00 alarm and a pre-booked taxi from the Old Town, or — the recommendation — moving to an airport hotel after dinner on the Friday. It is the single biggest structural decision on this page, so it has an appendix of its own: The last night →

The reason it matters more than a normal early flight: the morning of the 17th is not just a travel morning, it is the Irish border. She presents at Cork immigration under the Short Stay Visa Waiver Programme at around half eight, having to be calm, clear and organised with a folder of documents. Doing that on three hours’ sleep is a bad trade for one extra evening in a city-centre bed.

The flight is now booked and the time is fixed: Ryanair FR3732, EDI 07:05 to ORK 08:25, reference AV8L9S, both travellers on the one booking. The Friday below is timed to it.

📅  Three things to do this week

1. Check in for FR3732 now — the window is already open, and waiting until Friday is a needless risk. Ryanair opens online check-in 60 days before departure when a seat has been reserved, and seats 02B and 02C were paid for at booking. Sixty days before 17 October was 18 August, so it has been open for weeks. Do it from home, on a real connection, with the passports on the table.

Why it matters more than it looks: Jenifer is a non-EU/EEA passport holder, and Ryanair requires her travel document to be verified as part of check-in. That verification either succeeds in the app or it does not, and which of those happens changes what time the alarm goes off on Saturday. Finding that out five weeks early costs nothing. Finding it out at nine o’clock on Friday night in an airport hotel is the sort of thing that ruins a night’s sleep before a border. See Saturday morning → for both versions.

2. The Scottish school October break. Edinburgh’s schools usually take their October holiday in exactly this middle-of-the-month week, and if 2026 follows the pattern the city will be busier and dearer than a normal October, with the good mid-range rooms going first. Verify the City of Edinburgh Council holiday dates, then book the beds accordingly. York is unaffected — English half term is later in the month.

3. The baggage on the Cork flight — booked, but smaller than Qatar. A 20kg checked bag each went on at booking, which is the right call, but she arrived from Manila with 25kg and she is moving to Blarney until January. Weigh her bag on the Thursday, not at the desk on the Saturday: Ryanair sells extra weight far more cheaply in advance than at the airport, and an overweight bag at six in the morning on border day is the last thing you want. This is a scheduled job on Thursday evening →

Day 11
Arrive, and Don’t Rush
Wednesday, 14 October 2026  ·  In from York mid-afternoon
Half a day, and one fixed point. The train gets in at 14:39. Nothing today is booked except dinner, and the only thing that actually has a time on it is being on Calton Hill for the blue hour. 14:39 — out of Waverley

🚆  Waverley → 33 St Mary’s Street

🚂
Edinburgh Waverley is in a hole, and that is the whole trick to it The station sits at the bottom of a valley between the Old Town on one side and the New Town on the other, so you do not walk out of it, you climb out of it. There are three ways up and they go to different places: Market Street (Old Town, the low end), the Waverley Steps up to Princes Street (New Town, shops, trams), and the ramp up to the Royal Mile at the top. For the Travelodge, follow signs to Market Street and go up Cockburn Street — a curling Victorian street of record shops that delivers you onto the High Street. Turn right, walk down the Mile to the Netherbow, and St Mary’s Street is on your right. About ten minutes with bags, uphill the whole way. This is your first taste of the fact that Edinburgh is a city of stairs. If the bags are heavy or it is raining hard, there is a taxi rank inside the station on Waverley Bridge. Five minutes and about £8.

🏠  A village in a gorge, free
Dean Village
📍  Dean Village, Edinburgh EH4 3BU  ·  About 30 minutes on foot from St Mary’s Street
Free 🕐  Open all hours ⏱  Twenty minutes is plenty

A pocket of 19th-century millworkers’ houses tucked into a gorge below the level of the city. Ninety seconds off a main road and completely silent. Stand on the bridge and look back up the Water of Leith — it is the picture everyone takes, and it deserves it.

  • The route: west along the Royal Mile and down the Mound, along Princes Street to the west end, then down Queensferry Street and left at Bells Brae, which drops you into the gorge. It is downhill at the end, which is a relief.
  • Allow thirty minutes, not twenty. The twenty-minute figure you will read everywhere is measured from the west end of Princes Street. You are starting from the far end of the Royal Mile, which is another ten.
If the train ran late, skip this. Dean Village and the river are the droppable part of the afternoon. Calton Hill at sunset is not. If you are not walking out of the hotel by about 15:45, go straight to the hill and save this loop for a spare hour.

Sunset 18:15 — the best free hour in Edinburgh
⛰  Hill & monuments, free
Calton Hill
📍  Calton Hill, Edinburgh EH7 5AA  ·  10 minutes from the east end of Princes Street
Free 🕐  Open all hours ⏱  An hour, including the climb

Walk east along Princes Street and keep going onto Waterloo Place; about two minutes past the old Royal High School there is a wide stone staircase up on the left. It is a stepped path, not a hike. Five minutes and you are on a low, flat summit covered in improbable neoclassical monuments — including the National Monument, a half-finished Parthenon abandoned in 1829 when the money ran out, which Edinburgh has been quietly embarrassed about ever since.

  • You see everything at once. The whole Old Town skyline along the ridge with the castle at the end of it, Arthur’s Seat and Salisbury Crags to the south, the New Town’s grid below, and the Firth of Forth and Fife beyond. This is the postcard, and it costs nothing.
  • Arriving at ten past six is fine. Sunset is 18:15, but the good half hour is the one after the sun goes — when the castle floodlights come on and the sky is still deep blue. Dark is about 19:00. If the loop has run late, you have not missed it.
  • The Dugald Stewart Monument, the little round temple on the western edge, is the exact spot every photograph of Edinburgh is taken from. It frames the Old Town perfectly.
  • The Nelson Monument, the upturned telescope, can be climbed for a few pounds during the day — not needed, the hill itself is high enough.
Wear the coat you would wear on a boat. It is only 103 metres but it is completely exposed and the wind comes straight off the North Sea. This is the single most reliably freezing hour of the trip and it is worth being dressed for, because you will want to stay up there longer than you expect.

Day 12
Chocolate, a Volcano, a Secret Garden and the Oldest Pub in Scotland
Thursday, 15 October 2026  ·  The long day — and it is two clear days from the flight
This is the only day with real distance in it, and that is deliberate. It is put on the Thursday so there are two full days between the hard walking and a 05:15 alarm on border morning. The shape is simple: a slow start, then the park, then lunch at the far side of it, then a garden, then one bus home. Everything after the garden is downhill in the literal sense — you are back at the hotel by half four with your feet up.

The morning is deliberately empty, and that is the change. This day used to open with a 10:00 chocolate workshop, and the arithmetic did not work: 90 minutes of chocolate, fifteen down the Canongate, seventy-five up a volcano, twenty on the summit and forty back down puts you at the pub at ten past two, not half one. Chocolate-making is already booked in York on the Tuesday, so this one was doing the same job twice for £56 and costing the whole day its slack. It is gone. Have the lie-in, use the prepaid breakfast, and walk out at quarter past ten.

🌋  Extinct volcano, free
Arthur’s Seat & Holyrood Park
📍  Holyrood Park, Edinburgh EH8 8AZ  ·  Two routes, both ending in Duddingston
Free 🕐  Open all hours ⏱  2½ hours gate to pub, summit route

An extinct volcano in the middle of a capital city, 251 metres, and the summit view is the best in Scotland’s capital — the whole Old Town along its ridge one way, the Forth and Fife the other. Both routes below finish in the same place: Duddingston Village, on the far side, where lunch is. Pick one at the gate, based on the weather and the legs, and do not change your mind halfway.

  • The summit — budget 75 minutes up from the Holyrood gate, not the hour you will read everywhere. Steady climbing on a made path, then genuinely rough steps and bare rock for the last stretch. It is a scramble at the top, not a stroll, and the hour figure is a fit walker not stopping. Then 20 minutes actually standing on it — you did not climb a volcano to turn round — and 35 to 40 minutes down the eastern side into Duddingston. Descending rough ground is not faster than climbing smooth ground.
  • The gentler line is via Dunsapie Loch, where the final climb is only about twenty minutes — but you have to walk round Queen’s Drive to get to Dunsapie first, so it is kinder underfoot, not quicker. Same arrival time, less misery in the wet.
  • The flat way — about 40 minutes, almost no climbing. Skirt the base on the low path round the south side to Duddingston, with the crags above you the whole way. You are still properly out in it and it is a real walk, just without the summit. Take this one if it is raining, and do not feel short-changed.
The wind at the top is not a figure of speech. It regularly gusts hard enough to make the summit unpleasant and it has taken hats, hoods and phones off people. Nothing loose in your hands. The steps near the top are basalt and they are slick in rain — this is the one place on the trip where the shoes matter.
The timing, honestly, because the old version of this page did not add up. It had you leaving a chocolate workshop at 11:30, at the gate by 11:45, and at the pub for 13:30 — which quietly assumed a ten-minute walk that is fifteen, an hour’s climb that is seventy-five minutes, and no time at all standing on the summit. The real sum from the gate is 15 + 75 + 20 + 40 = 2 hours 30.

With the lie-in, it works with half an hour to spare: gate 10:30 → summit 11:45 → off the top 12:05 → Duddingston 12:45 → table at 13:30. That half hour is the leeway, and it is there so you can stop, take the photograph, and not look at a watch.

If you add a morning activity, move the table to 14:30. Do not try to hold 13:30 with something before it.
If you take the flat route, do the garden before lunch. The low path gets you to Duddingston well before one, which is an awkward hour to kill. Dr Neil’s Garden is open from 10:00 — walk round it first and eat at half one, and the day fits together properly.

🌱  Garden, free
Dr Neil’s Garden & Duddingston Loch
📍  Old Church Lane, Duddingston EH15 3PX  ·  Three minutes from the pub
Free 🕐  Daily, 10:00 to dusk ⏱  Forty-five minutes

Edinburgh’s secret garden, made from waste ground in the 1960s by two GPs, Andrew and Nancy Neil, who had their patients help dig it as therapy. It sits behind the 12th-century Duddingston Kirk on the shore of the loch, with Arthur’s Seat rising directly behind you — winding paths, a physic garden, Thomson’s Tower, benches facing the water, swans, and usually nobody else there at all.

  • It has almost no signage and you will walk past it. Aim for the Duddingston Kirk car park on Old Church Lane and follow the paved path down and round to the right. The gate sticks — shove it.
  • Dusk is the closing time and dusk is about 18:15. Not a constraint today, but worth knowing it is not a fixed hour.
  • The loch is a nature reserve, so the birds are the point — swans, herons and, in October, a great many geese making a great deal of noise.

🏰  Fortress, ticketed & timed
Edinburgh Castle
📍  Castlehill, Edinburgh EH1 2NG  ·  15 minutes up the Royal Mile from St Mary’s Street
💰  About £20–22 each, booked direct 🕐  09:30–17:00 in October, last entry 16:00 ⏱  Two to two and a half hours

Take the 09:30 slot and no other. Half nine to about eleven is by far the quietest window of the day, and you walk in while the queue is still forming on the Esplanade. The castle sits on a volcanic plug 130-odd metres above the city, and the thing to understand before you go in is that it is a fortress, not a palace — gun batteries, a garrison, a war memorial and a prison, arranged up a rock. A genuinely different animal from the Tower of London, which you have already done.

  • St Margaret’s Chapel — a single tiny room from about 1130, and the oldest building in Edinburgh. Twenty seconds to walk through and the best thing up there.
  • The Honours of Scotland in the Crown Room: the crown, sceptre and sword of state, the oldest crown jewels in Britain. Worth checking they are on display — the Crown Room has had closures for refurbishment.
  • Mons Meg, the six-tonne 15th-century siege bombard, and the Great Hall with its hammerbeam roof.
  • The Stone of Destiny is no longer here. It moved to the new Perth Museum in 2024 and it is not coming back. Most guidebooks and half the internet still say otherwise. Do not go looking for it.
  • The Scottish National War Memorial is the one people walk past and the one that lands hardest. Silent, and no photography.
  • The views off the north battlements are half the ticket — straight down over the New Town to the Forth.
Book direct with Historic Environment Scotland at edinburghcastle.scot. It is cheaper than the gate, it guarantees the slot, and a late slot is a bad slot — last entry is 16:00 and it shuts at 17:00, so there is no version of today where the afternoon works. Resellers charge a premium for the same ticket.

Scotland’s first cat café. A dozen rare-breed cats — ragdolls, Maine Coons, Norwegian Forest cats and a Sphynx — with the run of the place, and coffee and cake for you while they decide whether you are worth their attention.

📱  If the app will not check her in

The most likely reason is not a fault, and it is not a problem. Ryanair verifies non-EU/EEA travel documents as part of check-in, and that verification simply cannot be completed online for every nationality and route. When it cannot, Ryanair still checks you in — it issues a restricted boarding pass instead, and the document is verified by a human at the airport. Expect this rather than fear it. It is the ordinary path for a Philippine passport on a UK-to-Ireland flight.

What it changes: the alarm, and only the alarm. A restricted pass has to be taken to a staffed desk, and Ryanair asks you to be there at least two hours before departure — 05:05, not the 05:45 the timeline below assumes. So it is a 04:45 alarm and a 05:00 walk. That is the whole consequence. The desks open about two hours out and close strictly 40 minutes before departure, so the 06:25 cut-off does not move.

Do not pay to check in at the airport. Ryanair charges heavily — currently around £55 a head — for passengers who did not check in at all. A document check is not that. She is checked in; the pass is simply pending verification. If anyone suggests a fee, the answer is that check-in was completed online and this is a visa/document check. Have the confirmation email open.

The last night, and the alternatives

One decision that changes the shape of Friday, and a shelf of swap-ins for the days that do not go to plan.

Appendix A
The Last Night: Airport or Old Town
Friday 16 October, and the 07:05 on Saturday

Both versions work. The recommendation is the airport, and the reason is the border rather than the flight — but it is a genuine choice and the case for staying in town is not silly.

Recommended
Airport hotel, Friday night

05:15 alarm, ten-minute walk. Gelato in the Grassmarket at half four, bags collected at five, tram at 17:25, checked in by quarter past six. You keep the whole of Friday daytime in Edinburgh and trade only the last evening — which buys the folder hour, done warm and unhurried instead of at half nine with the bags still to move. Cost is roughly a wash: an airport room in October is no dearer than an Old Town one in a holiday week, and you save a £30 taxi.

The alternative
Stay in the Old Town

03:45 alarm, pre-booked taxi at 04:20, at the terminal by about 04:50. Do not rely on the tram: its first departure of the day is too late and too tight for a 07:05 flight. The Airlink 100 bus from Waverley Bridge runs through the night and is the reliable public option — about 30 minutes — but with three months of luggage a booked taxi is worth the money.

⚖️  What actually decides it

It is not the flight, it is what happens ninety minutes after the flight. A 4am start is survivable for anyone; people do it all the time. But at half past eight she has to stand at an immigration desk and be calm, clear, warm and organised in a conversation that determines whether the next three months happen. Sleep is not a luxury on that morning, it is preparation.

The second consideration is luggage. She is not going home after this — she is moving to Blarney until January, and everything she owns for that period is coming with her. Dragging that out of a city-centre hotel into a taxi at twenty past four in the dark, then out again at the terminal, is a materially worse morning than wheeling it across a car park at twenty to six.

Stay in town if: you find airport hotels genuinely depressing and the last night in a real city matters more to you than an hour and a half of sleep. That is a legitimate answer — just book the taxi the day before, and confirm it the night before.
