Monday, 12 October 2026  ·  Travel day from London
Morning, check out and travel

🚆  London to Edinburgh, LNER direct

🏠
Check out of the Premier Inn, Bankside Check-out is by noon. Pack the night before. Tube to King’s Cross: Jubilee line from Southwark, change at Waterloo for the Northern line to King’s Cross, about 25 minutes. Allow time to find the platform.
🚆
LNER, King’s Cross to Edinburgh Waverley Direct train, no changes. Journey time about 4hr 20min. Take a mid-morning departure to arrive early-to-mid afternoon. Advance fares open about 12 weeks ahead, so set a reminder for mid-July 2026. Seat tip: book a table seat on the east side and eat on board. The stretch along the Northumberland coast past Alnmouth and Berwick runs right along the cliff edge and is one of the great British rail journeys.
🚌
Edinburgh Waverley to the Radisson Blu The hotel is an 8-minute walk uphill from Waverley, on the Royal Mile itself. Exit at the Market Street exit and walk up through the Old Town closes, or up Waverley Bridge onto the Mile.

Day 10
The Castle & the Old Town
Tuesday, 13 October 2026
9:30am, pre-book, it sells out in October
🏛  Castle, pre-book timed entry
Edinburgh Castle
📍  Castlehill, Edinburgh EH1 2NG  ·  10-minute walk uphill from the hotel
💲  About £22pp  ·  Book: edinburghcastle.scot 🕐  Open 9:30am, arrive at opening ⏱  2.5 to 3 hours

Built on a volcanic rock above the city, the castle dominates Edinburgh from every angle. Arrive at opening. The Crown Jewels, the Stone of Destiny, St Margaret’s Chapel, and views from the ramparts across the Firth of Forth. The One O’Clock Gun fires at 1pm daily except Sunday.

  • The Honours of Scotland: the oldest crown jewels in the British Isles, the crown, sword and sceptre.
  • The Stone of Destiny: the ancient coronation stone, returned to Scotland in 1996.
  • St Margaret’s Chapel: the oldest building in Edinburgh, from about 1130.
  • The One O’Clock Gun: fired at 1pm from the Half Moon Battery. Stand on the esplanade at 12:58pm.
Strategy: arrive at opening to beat the queue for the Crown Jewels, which builds fast. Do the jewels first, then the Great Hall, then the ramparts.

⛪  Cathedral, free
St Giles’ Cathedral
📍  High Street, Edinburgh EH1 1RE  ·  On the Royal Mile, 5 minutes from the castle
Free  entry ⏱  30 to 45 minutes

The High Kirk of Edinburgh. The nave is impressive, but the Thistle Chapel inside is worth the small extra: a tiny, ornately carved chapel crammed with heraldic detail, Scotland’s equivalent of the Order of the Garter chapel at Windsor.

🏭  Streets & kirkyard, free
Victoria Street, The Vennel & Greyfriars Kirkyard
📍  Victoria St to Greyfriars, Edinburgh EH1 2QQ
Free ⏱  1 to 1.5 hours for all three

Three free stops close together, all worth the time.

  • Victoria Street: the curved, coloured street said to have inspired Diagon Alley. Walk slowly and look back up the curve from the Grassmarket end.
  • The Vennel: a stepped alley off the Grassmarket with the best castle photograph in the city, free and nearly empty.
  • Greyfriars Kirkyard: Greyfriars Bobby at the top of Candlemaker Row, and a lot of familiar surnames on the headstones.

Arthur’s Seat & One Spa
Wednesday, 14 October 2026
8:00am, sunrise about 7:35am
⛰  Hill walk, free
Arthur’s Seat
📍  Holyrood Park, Edinburgh EH8 8HG  ·  Start from Holyrood, at the foot of the Mile
Free 🕐  About 45 minutes up the main path ⏱  2 to 2.5 hours round trip

An extinct volcano in the middle of the city, 251 metres, about 45 minutes up the main path from Holyrood. Views over Edinburgh, the Forth and the Pentlands, the finest in the city. Sunrise is about 7:35am so an 8am start gets good light.

Bring a windproof layer: it is always windy at the top, and colder than the forecast. Waterproof footwear is worth it, the path can be damp.

Afternoon, free, the loveliest walk in the city
🌿  Riverside walk, free
Water of Leith Walkway to Dean Village
📍  Start: Stockbridge, EH4 1LR  ·  End: Dean Village, EH4 3BQ
Free 🕐  Open at all times ⏱  About 45 minutes at an easy pace

The path drops into a hidden gorge below the New Town and the city vanishes completely. St Bernard’s Well is on the way, and Dean Village at the end is a former mill village almost no visitor finds. Quietly one of the loveliest walks in any British city.

  • St Bernard’s Well: a Roman-style mineral spring pavilion from 1789 on the riverbank.
  • Dean Village: 17th-century mill buildings around a medieval bridge. The view from Dean Bridge looking down is the one everybody photographs.
If it rains: swap this for the National Museum of Scotland if you have not already been, or simply do it in drizzle, the gorge is atmospheric either way.
