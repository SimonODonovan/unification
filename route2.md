## 2. Hard constraints — re-stated and re-verified
 
Every permutation below has to live inside these constraints. Where the user's framing in conversation diverged from the constraint, that's flagged explicitly.
 
### 2.1 UK Standard Visitor Visa
- **Validity:** 6 months from issue (the first-time-applicant default). The visa is a **single 6-month window**, not "6 months of UK time within a longer window."
- **Per-visit cap:** 6 months max per entry.
- **Multi-entry:** the standard 6-month visa is multi-entry by default — J can leave and re-enter UK during the validity window.
- **No "main home" in UK:** the immigration rules forbid making UK J's primary residence. Long single stays are scrutinised more than short visits, but a single 6-month stay is permitted on its face.
- **Critical exclusion for SSVWP:** the visa **cannot be issued "for visitors seeking to marry or to enter a civil partnership."** A UK Marriage Visitor Visa **disqualifies** the SSVWP onward route. **Apply for and frame as a general visitor — tourism + family visit. Do not mention marriage anywhere in the UK application or in supporting correspondence.** The Belfast itinerary day already flags this.
- **Activation requirement for SSVWP:** UK ETAs are not enough — Filipino nationals need the actual visa stamp. J needs to physically land in UK and be admitted before SSVWP-crossing to Ireland is lawful.
### 2.2 Northern Ireland is the United Kingdom
- Time spent in NI counts toward the **UK** 6-month visa.
- Time spent in NI does **not** count toward the Irish SSVWP 90-day allowance.
- Belfast / Newry / Derry are all UK soil for immigration purposes. The trick for Stage 1 is that NI is *also* close enough to ROI's border-hub towns that S can commute daily across the CTA land border for work — see §2.5.
### 2.3 Ireland's Short Stay Visa Waiver Programme (SSVWP)
- Philippine nationals **are eligible** (verified ISD page).
- **Stay cap per Irish entry: 90 days, or the remaining UK permission, whichever is less.**
- **Fresh UK landing required for each Irish entry** — J cannot bounce PH → Ireland directly on a UK visa, nor can she re-enter Ireland from PH mid-trip. Every Irish entry must be preceded by a UK arrival.
- **Multiple Irish entries are permitted** within UK visa validity, each capped at 90 days. The cumulative Irish total is implicitly bounded by the UK visa's 6-month window.
### 2.4 Critical maths — "9 months" is not a thing on a 6-month visa
**The user's conversation framing of "3 months UK + 3 months NI + 3 months ROI" totals 9 months and exceeds the 6-month UK visa validity.** The visa is a single 6-month window. Realistic interpretations:
 
- **6 months UK total**, of which Britain (England/Scotland) and NI can be split any way → no SSVWP trip on this visit.
- **~3 months UK + ~3 months ROI** (split however) → all-in 6 months across both jurisdictions. ROI cannot exceed 90 days on any single SSVWP entry.
- **Intermediate splits** — e.g., 4 months UK + 60 days ROI — work fine.
A *longer-term UK visa* (2-year, 5-year, 10-year multi-entry) would extend the window across years, but each visit is still capped at 6 months, and first-time PH applicants are typically issued the 6-month visa even if a longer one is requested. **See Option F for the 2-year-application hedge.**
 
### 2.5 S's "work from Ireland only" rule
- Oomnitza's policy: S works **only from Ireland**. Confirmed in [[feedback_simon_border_hub]] memory.
- The workable pattern: **sleep NI, work ROI**, by daily car commute across the open CTA land border. Two viable pairings:
  - **Newry (NI, BT34) ↔ Dundalk (ROI):** ~30-min drive, easiest. Newry has decent rental stock; Dundalk has coworking (e.g., Block T, Regional Development Centre) and is on the M1 corridor.
  - **Derry/Londonderry (NI) ↔ Letterkenny (ROI):** ~30-min drive, equally viable for the western flank. Letterkenny coworking less abundant but adequate.
- **Belfast as base is borderline.** Belfast ↔ Drogheda is 1h15m each way — feasible for an occasional day, painful as a daily 5-day-a-week pattern. If urban amenities for J during S's working hours matter, the trade-off is 2.5h commute/day; if not, **Newry is the better base**.
- **Holiday and ROI legs are fine for S's work continuity** — annual leave covers the 2-week holiday; the ROI leg is normal work from Blarney.
### 2.6 Land borders kill passport-stamp evidence
- The CTA land border (NI ↔ ROI) has **no checkpoint** and **no passport stamping**.
- The user's stated evidence goal — "passport stamps upon entry to demonstrate compliance" — means every UK↔ROI transition that needs to be evidenced **must happen by air** (or, in principle, by ferry with a stamping officer, but air is the realistic default).
- **Daily border commute by S** is fine because he is an Irish citizen exercising CTA rights and isn't trying to evidence anything. Daily commute by S does not affect J's stamps.
- **J's transitions** between UK and ROI for the trip-sequencing legs should always be by air, with appropriate airline check-in and passport control.
### 2.7 HSE 3-month marriage notice does **not** apply to Stage 1
The marriage happens in PH (Stage 2), not Ireland. The HSE notice mechanic in `C_Visa_Marriage_Route_Plan.md` §2 is relevant to *Route 3* (Irish wedding). For Route 2 it can be ignored for Stage 1 entirely; the Irish marriage register does not enter the picture until Track B at Stage 3.

Each permutation is a sequencing of three blocks: **Britain holiday (14d)**, **NI base (variable)**, **ROI SSVWP (≤90d)**. Total has to fit in 180 days of UK visa validity. All cross-jurisdiction transitions are by **air** so passport stamps document the chain.

### 3.6 Option F — Apply for a 2-year multi-entry UK visa instead
 
This is **a meta-choice**, not a separate sequencing — it pairs with whichever of A/B/D you pick. The 2-year multi-entry UK Standard Visitor Visa (~£186 vs ~£127 for 6-month) extends the validity window across two years while keeping the 6-month-per-visit cap. The benefit isn't more time on this trip — it's the **ability to make a second 6-month visit to UK + ROI during Stage 2/3 if anything goes sideways**.
 
Realistic outcome for a first-time PH applicant: the caseworker often issues a 6-month visa even when a longer one is requested, because there's no demonstrated UK travel history. The £59 delta is small enough that **applying for the 2-year and accepting whatever is granted is a free option**.

## 5. Rank-ordered recommendation
 
### 1st — Option A (Britain holiday → NI base → ROI SSVWP → PH) ★ PRIMARY
 
Best balance of evidence quality, operational simplicity, and downstream positioning. NI commute is front-loaded so S finds out early whether the border-hub pattern is sustainable; if it isn't, you can compress NI and start the ROI leg sooner. Final leg is the ROI SSVWP, which means Blarney cohabitation is the freshest material in the file when J flies home, and the most recent passport stamp is a clean DUB → MNL exit, perfectly aligned with the Stage 3 Irish C-visa application. Matches the existing live itinerary almost exactly (just appends the NI base and ROI leg after the published 14-day finish in Belfast).
 
**Shortfalls / risks specific to Option A:**
- **NI commute risk hits first.** If Newry-Dundalk doesn't work for S, you've committed to it for 76 days before discovering. Mitigation: dry-run a single week of the commute in **late June or July 2026** before locking the NI rental.
- **Winter NI weather.** Late Sep through early Dec in NI gets dark, wet, and grim. J is from PH — manage expectations.
- **Tight visa-buffer at the end.** Visa expires 5 Mar 2027; departure 4 Mar 2027. Build a 2-day buffer (depart 2 Mar) instead of riding the edge — flight disruption could create an overstay.
### 2nd — Option B (Britain holiday → ROI SSVWP → NI base → PH) — strong alternative
 
Same total evidence weight as A, ordering inverted. Three reasons B might be the better pick:
- **Joint accounts / utility / mail at Blarney get established early in the trip**, so by Track B file-build at Stage 3, the joint-occupation paper trail is 5–6 months deep instead of 2–3 months.
- **S's commute burden is back-loaded** — easier to absorb after 3 months of normal work pattern at Blarney.
- **Winter is in NI**, which is no worse than Option A but symmetric — and the spring ROI months are missed (which is fine for A's compliance maths, but B uses warm Irish autumn instead).
**Shortfalls / risks specific to Option B:**
- **The 14-day holiday loses the Belfast leg** as currently published — the EDI → BFS → 3 days flight + drive segment becomes EDI → DUB direct. The current public itinerary at simonodonovan.github.io needs an edit. Not a big deal — Belfast can be folded into the NI base leg.
- **Most recent passport stamp is BFS → MNL** at trip end, not a clean DUB exit. The Embassy may notice and ask a question; truthful answer is "we spent the last leg in NI to support my partner's work." Minor friction.
- **S has 90 consecutive days of normal Blarney work upfront** — actually a pro, not a con, but it does mean if Oomnitza tightens any policy mid-trip, the NI commute portion comes when J's UK visa is nearer its expiry and there's less rope for a course-correction.
### 3rd — Option D (all UK, no ROI) — fallback if SSVWP framing carries unwanted risk
 
If at any point you decide the SSVWP / "UK visa as backdoor to Ireland" framing is too risky for the eventual Irish C-visa application — i.e., if Embassy Manila is likely to read it as "she's been positioning to settle in Ireland via the back door" — then D is the cleaner story. J had a UK visa, she stayed in UK (NI), she went home. No SSVWP, no IE stamps, no narrative for the Embassy to second-guess.
 
**Shortfalls / risks specific to Option D:**
- **No Blarney cohabitation evidence pre-marriage**. Joint utility, joint mail, joint account at Blarney all wait until post-wedding (Stage 3). The Track B file is materially weaker on the "residing together in the same household" criterion.
- **Heaviest commute load on S** — 24 weeks of cross-border driving 5x/week. Real ergonomic problem; consider a Belfast-Drogheda pattern with hybrid (3 days commute / 2 days WFH-from-NI surreptitiously) — the latter would be a policy breach so don't bank on it.
- **Wastes the SSVWP option.** Filipino SSVWP access is a meaningful entitlement; using it builds the kind of compliance history that helps future applications. D forgoes that.
### 4th — Option E (just the published holiday) — emergency baseline only
 
Use only if (a) UK visa is refused on the longer-stay plan and you re-apply for the short version, or (b) something material changes in S's job that makes the extended stay untenable. E is a *retreat position*, not a planned outcome.
 
### 5th — Option C (split SSVWP) — over-engineered, do not pursue
 
The only genuine benefit of C is the "demonstrated pattern of return" via two Irish entry/exit pairs. Options A and B already produce a clean Irish exit; the marginal benefit of a second pair is small and the operational cost is large (4 air transitions, 4 accommodations, twice the date-tracking discipline). Skip.
 
### Cross-cutting recommendation — pair with Option F (2-year visa application)
 
Regardless of the sequencing pick, **apply for the 2-year multi-entry UK Standard Visitor Visa** (~£186) rather than the 6-month (~£127). For a £59 premium, you preserve the option of a second UK + ROI trip during Stages 2/3 — useful insurance if the Irish C visa is delayed and S/J want another stretch together pre-Track B. Worst case: caseworker still issues a 6-month visa, no harm done.

## 12. Open questions worth flagging
 
- **Where exactly does J get her UK entry stamped on arrival from MNL?** If she connects through Doha / Dubai / Abu Dhabi, she lands at LHR — that's the UK entry. If she takes a routing that has her transit through an EU airport (e.g., AMS), the UK entry is still LHR. Confirm the flight routing is direct-to-UK with no Schengen transit complications.
- **Does S need to disclose to Oomnitza** that he's sleeping in NI while working from ROI? Probably not — the working location is in ROI and that's what the policy cares about. But it's worth a 1:1 conversation with his manager to flag the arrangement in writing rather than discover a policy interpretation issue mid-trip.
- **Does the Newry rental need to be in J's name for evidence?** No — S being the named tenant and J being a co-occupant on a signed cover email is sufficient for "residing in the same household" purposes at Track B. But naming J on the rental agreement directly is strictly stronger; ask the landlord.
- **Should they apply for a separate Irish 'C' visa now as a backup** in case the UK visa is delayed? Probably not — the UK visa is the easier gate (86% vs Irish C visa discretionary), the SSVWP route depends on the UK visa anyway, and applying for both risks confused signalling. Hold the Irish C visa for Stage 3 post-marriage where it's the centerpiece of the plan.
- **Wedding format / location preference in PH** — civil only vs civil + Catholic vs civil + family reception. Affects Stage 2 timeline length. Not Stage 1 dependent.
