## 2. Hard constraints
 
### 2.1 UK Standard Visitor Visa
- **Validity:** 6 months from issue (default for first-time PH applicants), single window.
- **Per-visit cap:** 6 months max per entry; multi-entry by default.
- **No "main home" in UK** under the immigration rules; a single 6-month stay is on its face permitted.
- **🚨 Cannot be a Marriage Visitor Visa** — that class is an explicit SSVWP exclusion. Apply for and frame the application as a general visitor — tourism + family visit. **No mention of marriage anywhere in the UK application or supporting correspondence.** The PH wedding happens *after* this trip; UK visa application is silent on it.
- **UK ETAs do not qualify for SSVWP.** Filipinos still require a visa, so this is moot, but worth noting that the issued document must be the visa stamp, not an ETA.
### 2.2 Northern Ireland is the UK
- Time in NI counts toward the UK 6-month visa.
- Time in NI does **not** count toward Irish SSVWP days.
- Belfast / Newry / Derry are all UK soil for immigration purposes.
### 2.3 Ireland's Short Stay Visa Waiver Programme (SSVWP) — single-entry mechanics
Per the official ISD page (last updated 2025-05-09) and the [SSVWP information PDF](https://www.irishimmigration.ie/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Irish-Short-Stay-Visa-Waiver-Programme-February-2023.pdf):
- Philippine nationals are eligible.
- Maximum stay per entry: **90 days, or remaining UK permission, whichever is the lesser amount of time.**
- Must have landed in the UK on the current UK visa *before* travelling to Ireland.
- Cannot enter Ireland directly on the UK visa for the first journey from home.
- **★ MANDATORY land-border reporting requirement (verified 2026-05-23 from the ISD PDF) ★:** *"If you arrive in Ireland via the border with Northern Ireland, you must report to an Immigration Officer at Burgh Quay, Registration Office, Dublin or local Immigration Office at a Garda Síochána (police) station as soon as possible."* This means a car/land crossing from NI into ROI is **not** an off-the-books entry — it triggers an obligation to present in person at an Immigration Office, where the officer records the entry and (in practice) stamps the passport. The land crossing is therefore evidenced after all; the stamp just gets put in later at the reporting office rather than at the border. This is a material correction to the v1/v2 framing of Option A.
### 2.4 SSVWP multiple entries — yes, allowed, with caveats
 
**Yes, your interpretation is correct.** The ISD page explicitly contemplates more than one Irish entry within a single UK visa validity. The relevant ISD wording:
 
> "if you have already been to the UK and then travelled home, but still have time remaining on your UK permission, then you can travel directly to Ireland provided the visit is within remaining time on your UK permission."
 
> "Each distinct period of leave to remain in the UK (up to a maximum of 180 days each time) requires a prior legal entry into the UK before travel to Ireland under the programme, no matter what the duration of the UK visa."
 
Translation in plain language:
1. **Multiple Irish entries are permitted** within the UK visa validity window.
2. **Each Irish entry is independently capped at 90 days** *or* the remaining UK permission time, whichever is lesser. ISD's worked example: a person with 180 days of UK permission who has already burned 160 of them can only enter Ireland for 20 days — not 90 — on the next SSVWP entry.
3. **Each Irish entry must be preceded by a legal entry into the UK.** This is what lets a second SSVWP entry work: you must come back to UK soil between Irish stays. The border re-entry can be a flight (preferred for stamp evidence) or a land crossing from ROI into NI.
**Practical answer to your scenario** (14d UK holiday → 90d IE → 1 wk UK → second IE leg):
- 14 days UK holiday (Day 1–14)
- 89 days IE SSVWP #1 (Day 15–103) ★ stay under 90 for buffer
- 7 days UK (Day 104–110) ★ re-establishes UK presence
- IE SSVWP #2 (Day 111–167) — capped at min(90, remaining-UK-permission). Remaining permission on Day 111 is 180-111 = 69 days. So SSVWP #2 ≤ 69 days. With a 14-day visa-expiry buffer, IE #2 can run ~58 days.
That's the Option C in §3.3 below. It is **mechanically permitted**, but it carries two flavours of discretionary risk:
1. **Border-officer scrutiny on second SSVWP entry.** Two back-to-back ~90-day stays in 6 months can be read as soft migration. The IBOS officer at the second entry has discretion to refuse on "non-genuine short-stay" grounds. J should be ready to evidence purpose and ties to PH at the second entry.
2. **The 7-day UK gap must be a real, demonstrable UK presence** — not a paper exercise. A land border crossing for one night and back to ROI would likely be treated as not having re-entered UK in any meaningful sense.
Sources cited: [ISD SSVWP page](https://www.irishimmigration.ie/coming-to-visit-ireland/short-stay-visa-waiver-programme/) (last updated 9 May 2025); [ISD SSVWP information PDF 2023](https://www.irishimmigration.ie/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Irish-Short-Stay-Visa-Waiver-Programme-February-2023.pdf).
 
### 2.5 Passport-stamp reality — corrected from v1
 
V1 of this document made several incorrect stamping assumptions. The corrected picture:
 
| Transition | Stamp produced? | Notes |
| - | - | - |
| MNL → LHR (UK entry by air) | **YES** (UK entry stamp at LHR) | UK Border Force stamps non-CTA visa nationals on first entry |
| Internal UK flights (e.g., LHR → EDI, EDI → BFS) | No (internal UK) | No further UK stamping |
| EDI/LHR/MAN → DUB (UK → IE by air) | **YES** (Irish entry stamp at DUB) | IBOS stamps visa-required nationals at Dublin Airport even on intra-CTA flights |
| DUB/BFS → MNL (Final departure by air) | **No exit stamp** (Ireland and UK do not routinely stamp exits) | Departure is captured digitally via airline API to Border Force / Garda; not visible in passport |
| ROI → NI by car (land border crossing) | **No stamp at the border**, but no SSVWP-side reporting obligation in this direction | Direction not regulated by SSVWP; UK side has no equivalent reporting requirement for Filipinos who already hold a valid UK visa |
| NI → ROI by car (land border crossing) | **No stamp at the border, BUT SSVWP requires J to report to an Immigration Officer at Burgh Quay (Dublin) or a local Garda Síochána Immigration Office "as soon as possible" after entry.** In practice the officer records the entry and stamps the passport at that visit. | **Mandatory** per the SSVWP PDF. Non-compliance is a regulatory breach. The post-entry stamp is the SSVWP entry stamp for evidence purposes. |
| Scheduled flights Belfast ↔ Dublin | **None operate.** | No regular commercial flights BFS ↔ DUB. Both routes serve common transfer airports (Manchester, London, Edinburgh, Birmingham) — but no direct BFS-DUB or DUB-BFS service. **The user is correct: any NI ↔ ROI movement is by car (or bus, train, ferry — all land/sea CTA, no stamps).** |
 
**Implications for evidence:**
- **Guaranteed stamp events:** UK entry at LHR; Irish entry at DUB *if J arrives by air*; UK re-entry at MAN/LHR/etc. *if J flies into UK from outside UK*.
- **Land entry to ROI is also a stamped event — but the stamp gets applied at the Burgh Quay or Garda-station report visit, not at the border itself.** ISD mandates this report. So the entry date is captured officially regardless of whether J flies into DUB or drives in.
- **No exit-side stamps from either UK or IE** — departures are evidenced by boarding pass + airline manifest + (later) digital border records.
- **Day-counting under SSVWP** is therefore fully defensible in either Option A (land entry + Burgh Quay/Garda report) or Option B (air entry stamped at DUB). The earlier v2 framing that A lacked stamp evidence was wrong — *the stamp arrives via a different mechanism*. The remaining differential is the **timing** of the stamp (immediate on entry for B; "as soon as possible" for A — practically same-day or within 1–2 days), and the **administrative overhead** (B's stamping is automatic at airport immigration; A requires a deliberate visit to Burgh Quay or Cork Anglesea Street Garda Station).

## 10. Dublin entry framing — the brief for J at the IBOS desk
 
Independent of which option is picked, on any SSVWP entry J will face an Immigration Bureau (IBOS) officer at Dublin Airport asking the standard questions. **The right approach is truthful, brief, and unambiguous about purpose and duration. Don't volunteer extras.**
 
### 10.1 Why "I'm here for 2 weeks of tourism" is the wrong answer
 
If J says "2 weeks" but stays 80+ days, two things go wrong:
1. **At Stage 3 C visa application**, the Embassy Manila has access to her travel history. A 2-week declaration followed by an 80+ day stay reads as misrepresentation at first entry. That is *materially worse* for the Embassy than a truthful 80+ day declaration.
2. **At later UK or Irish visa applications**, IBOS or UKVI may reference the entry record. Any discrepancy between declared intent and actual stay is a red flag.
**Misrepresentation at entry is a much sharper risk than honest declaration of a long visit under SSVWP.** SSVWP exists precisely to allow up to 90 days of visit. Using most of it is not abnormal.
 
### 10.2 The recommended framing (truthful, complete, brief)
 
When asked "How long are you staying?" — **"Up to 90 days. I'm visiting my partner Simon, who is an Irish citizen and lives in Blarney, near Cork. My return flight to Manila is on [date]."**
 
When asked "What is your purpose?" — **"Family visit. Simon is my partner; I'm staying with him at his home and meeting his family."**
 
When asked "What do you do?" — Truthful: she's recently resigned from IBM PH; she has decided to take this trip with her partner before considering next steps in her career. *Do not volunteer marriage plans unless asked specifically.*
 
When asked "Have you been to Ireland before?" — Truthful (yes, on the 2025 visit visa).
 
When asked specifically about future plans / marriage — **"We have talked about getting married, possibly in the Philippines later this year. The wedding isn't here in Ireland."** This is true and frames the SSVWP stay correctly: she is *not* here to marry. Marriage will happen in PH, after the SSVWP visit, before Stage 3.
 
### 10.3 The user's proposed framing ("changed plans, will marry in PH, want to spend time planning with family in IE") — assessment
 
This framing is *truthful* and *consistent*. The risk profile is:
- **Pro:** Explains a long stay in a way Border Force will read as benign (visiting partner, no Irish marriage intent, real PH wedding planned).
- **Con:** Volunteering "marriage planning" into the entry conversation invites follow-up questions and may extend the entry interview (longer interview ≠ refusal, but more scrutiny).
**Recommended posture:** be ready to give that framing if asked specifically about marriage, but do not lead with it. Lead with "visiting my partner, up to 90 days, returning to MNL on [date]."
