Day 1 4 Oct Sunday
Arrival & South Bank
DaytimeJenifer flies out of Manila on 4 Oct and arrives at Heathrow either later the same evening or in the early hours of 5 Oct, depending on the routing booked. Simon arrives from Cork on 4 Oct. Both take the Elizabeth Line directly into central London — simplest connection, no changes. Check in, drop bags, get settled.
EveningIf the arrival lands early enough, a short South Bank walk to stretch the legs — Millennium Bridge and the riverfront. Dinner at a riverside restaurant. Early night; it is a very long travel day for Jenifer. If the flight arrives overnight, this all shifts to the morning of 5 Oct instead.

Day 2 5 Oct Monday
Westminster & the Natural History Museum
MorningWestminster walk. Start at Westminster Bridge for the Parliament and Big Ben view. St James’s Park — pelicans, deck chairs, the palace at the far end. Buckingham Palace exterior, Horse Guards Parade. No rush; there is nothing timed today.
LunchHead north through Soho — Wardour Street or Greek Street for a relaxed lunch.
AfternoonNatural History Museum, South Kensington (free, 2–2.5hrs). District line four stops from Westminster. Hintze Hall and the blue whale skeleton, the dinosaur gallery, Earth Hall and the volcanoes and earthquakes section. The building itself is worth the visit — a Victorian cathedral to the natural world.
EveningTube back east to Covent Garden for a wander and dinner — Covent Garden and Seven Dials have a wide range of good restaurants at every price point.
Natural History Museum — freeDistrict line: Westminster → South Kensington, ~10min
Day 3 6 Oct Tuesday
East London
MorningTower Bridge & the Thames. Walk from London Bridge station. Tower Bridge exterior and the riverfront east past St Katharine Docks. Good views back across to the City skyline.
LunchBrick Lane. A salt beef bagel from Beigel Bake at the north end of the street — open 24hrs, a genuine London institution. Or one of the many cafes along the road.
AfternoonShoreditch & Spitalfields. Street art around Hanbury Street and Princelet Street. Spitalfields Market for a browse. This area rewards slow walking with no fixed plan.
EveningDinner in Shoreditch — one of London’s best areas for independent restaurants.
Beigel Bake — north end of Brick Lane, open 24hrsOverground from Shoreditch High St
Day 4 7 Oct Wednesday
Bloomsbury & Borough Market
MorningBritish Museum (free — arrive at opening, 10am). The Great Court is remarkable before the crowds arrive. Choose one or two wings to focus on rather than rushing everywhere. Allow 2.5hrs minimum.
Late AMGrant Museum of Zoology (free, a 10-min walk north from the British Museum). One of the last university zoology collections left in London: skeletons, jars, and the famous drawer of moles, packed into a single Victorian room. Small enough for 45 minutes and completely unlike the big museums.
LunchWalk south across the river to Borough Market for lunch. Open Wednesdays — a wide range of stalls. This is the meal of the day; take time with it.
AfternoonRelaxed South Bank walk east from Borough — Millennium Bridge, Shakespeare’s Globe exterior, the riverfront. A good hour’s wander with no agenda.
EveningDinner in Bermondsey or back in Soho. Early start is not needed tomorrow — the Windsor trip works best mid-morning.
British Museum — freeGrant Museum of Zoology — freeCheck Grant Museum opening hours — limited
Day 5 8 Oct Thursday
Windsor Castle Day Trip
MorningTake the Elizabeth Line from Paddington to Windsor & Eton Central — about 40 minutes direct. Windsor Castle is the oldest and largest occupied castle in the world — home to the Royal Family for over 900 years. Pre-book timed entry. St George’s Chapel inside the castle grounds is one of the finest examples of Gothic architecture in England. Allow 2.5–3hrs for the castle.
LunchLunch in Windsor town — the streets around the castle have a good range of cafes and restaurants. The town is small and very easy to walk.
AfternoonWalk along the Long Walk — a three-mile avenue of trees stretching from the castle gates into Windsor Great Park. You do not need to walk the whole thing; even 20–30 minutes along it gives a sense of the extraordinary scale. Or explore the town itself before heading back.
EveningTrain back to London (~40 min). Quiet evening; one extra day in London tomorrow before moving north.
Pre-book Windsor Castle timed entryElizabeth Line to Windsor: ~40min from PaddingtonLong Walk & Windsor Great Park — free
Day 6 9 Oct Friday
Greenwich & the Thames by Boat
MorningTake the Thames Clipper from Bankside Pier — a 5-minute walk from the hotel — east to Greenwich. The river journey itself is one of the great London views: past the City skyline, Canary Wharf, the O2. Royal Observatory & the Prime Meridian (pre-book). The hilltop position gives panoramic views over the city and the Isle of Dogs. Allow 1.5hrs.
LunchGreenwich Market for lunch — a covered market with a wide range of street food stalls running throughout the week. One of the most varied lunchtime spots in London and a good browse.
AfternoonCutty Sark (pre-book separately) — the last surviving Victorian tea clipper, fully restored and displayed on a glass base in the original dry dock. An extraordinary piece of maritime engineering worth a slow hour. Boat back to Bankside or DLR to London Bridge.
EveningLast London dinner. Pack bags tonight — the train to York leaves tomorrow morning.
Thames Clipper: Bankside → Greenwich ~40minPre-book Royal ObservatoryPre-book Cutty Sark

Day 7 10 Oct Saturday
Arrive York — Medieval Streets
MorningCheck out of the London hotel. Tube to Kings Cross and board the LNER north. ~2hrs to York — a comfortable journey. Arrive early afternoon.
AfternoonCheck in at the hotel (5-min walk from the station). Head straight to The Shambles — the most famous medieval street in England, so narrow in places the overhanging buildings nearly touch. No ticket, no queue. Walk the surrounding lanes: Whip-Ma-Whop-Ma-Gate, the Shambles Market, the snickleways (York’s network of tiny alleyways).
Late PMWalk the City Walls — a free 3km circuit of the original Roman and medieval walls around the city. The stretch from Bootham Bar to Monk Bar has the best views over the Minster. Allow 1–1.5hrs.
EveningDinner in York. Gillygate and Micklegate have a good range of independent restaurants.
The Shambles — freeCity Walls walk — freeBook advance train: £20–40pp
Day 8 11 Oct Sunday
York Minster, Museums & Betty’s
MorningYork Minster. Pre-book the tower climb for a panoramic view over the whole city — it is the best vantage point in York. The cathedral itself is one of the finest Gothic buildings in Northern Europe; allow 1.5hrs inside without hurrying.
Late AMNational Railway Museum (free, a 10-min walk from the Minster). One of the genuinely great free museums in Britain — the Flying Scotsman, a Japanese bullet train, the royal carriages. Allow 1.5hrs; it is more engaging than it sounds.
LunchBetty’s Tea Room on St Helen’s Square. Expect a queue of 15–20 minutes — it moves steadily and is worth every minute. A proper Yorkshire institution that has been in the same building since 1936.
AfternoonRelaxed afternoon in the city. Clifford’s Tower (the remains of York’s Norman castle, with views over the city) is worth the short climb. Or a slow walk along the River Ouse and the riverside gardens.
EveningDinner in York. Gillygate and Micklegate for independent restaurants.
National Railway Museum — freePre-book York Minster tower climbBetty’s: short queue, absolutely worth it
Day 9 12 Oct Monday
Jorvik Viking Centre & Museum Gardens
MorningJorvik Viking Centre (pre-book a morning slot). Below the modern shopping street of Coppergate lies the preserved site of a Viking-age neighbourhood, excavated in the 1970s and now explored by a slow-moving vehicle through a recreation of 10th-century Jorvik. Built on the actual excavation site — the authenticity is remarkable. Allow 1.5hrs.
LunchRelaxed lunch in the city — Goodramgate or Gillygate for independent restaurants and cafes. York is quiet and very pleasant on a Monday.
AfternoonYorkshire Museum & Museum Gardens (free entry to the gardens; museum entry optional). The gardens hold the open-air ruins of St Mary’s Abbey — a quiet, photogenic spot that most visitors miss entirely. The Museum itself covers Roman York, Viking York, and medieval Yorkshire.
EveningFinal York dinner. Pack bags tonight — the Edinburgh train leaves Monday morning.
Pre-book Jorvik Viking CentreMuseum Gardens — freeYorkshire Museum — free (selected times)

Arrive Edinburgh — First Evening on the Royal Mile
MorningTrain from York. ~2hrs 30min, arriving Edinburgh Waverley early-to-mid afternoon. The hotel is a 10-minute walk up the Royal Mile from the station.

Day 11 14 Oct Wednesday
Edinburgh Castle & Old Town
MorningEdinburgh Castle. Pre-book a timed entry and arrive at opening. The Crown Jewels, the Stone of Destiny, the One O’Clock Gun. The views from the castle ramparts over the city, Arthur’s Seat, and out to the Firth of Forth are extraordinary. Allow 2.5–3hrs — there is considerably more to see here than most people expect.
LunchDescend the Royal Mile for lunch at one of the older pubs just off the Mile — The Jolly Judge (down a close near the castle) is a quiet, unpretentious option away from the main tourist flow.
AfternoonOld Town wander down to Chambers Street: St Giles’ Cathedral (free), Victoria Street (the curved, colourful street said to have inspired Diagon Alley), and Greyfriars Kirkyard. Then the National Museum of Scotland (free) next door — the natural history floors are the reason to go: Animal World with its suspended skeletons, the T. rex cast, Dolly the Sheep, and the vast Victorian Grand Gallery. Allow 2hrs.
EveningCalton Hill at sunset. A 5-minute walk from the east end of Princes Street — a short climb to a hilltop with 360° views over the entire city. One of Edinburgh’s best kept free secrets. October light on a clear evening is remarkable.
Pre-book Edinburgh Castle: sells out in OctoberNational Museum of Scotland — freeGreyfriars Kirkyard — freeCalton Hill — free
Day 12 15 Oct Thursday
Arthur’s Seat & One Spa
Early AMArthur’s Seat. Start by 8am before the day gets busy. The main path from Holyrood is clearly marked and not technically difficult — about 45 minutes to the summit at a comfortable pace. The views from the top over Edinburgh, the Firth of Forth, and the Pentland Hills are extraordinary on a clear October morning. Bring a layer; it is always windy at the top.
Late AMWalk back down past Holyrood Palace and the free-to-view Holyrood Abbey ruins. Coffee at the foot of the Royal Mile — well earned after the hike.
AfternoonOne Spa at the Sheraton Grand, Festival Square (day access, no hotel stay needed). The rooftop hydropool looks straight at the castle, and there is a full thermal suite below it. Book 2–3 weeks ahead and book treatments for both at the same time. The single best-earned afternoon of the trip after a morning on the hill.
EveningPrinces Street Gardens and the New Town on the walk back. Dinner on George Street.
Arthur’s Seat — freeBook One Spa 2–3 weeks aheadGood walking shoes for the hike
Day 13 16 Oct Friday
Britannia, then south to London
MorningCheck out and leave bags at the hotel. Bus 16 or the tram down to Leith, about 20 minutes. Royal Yacht Britannia (pre-book the first slot; allow 2hrs). The royal family’s private floating home from 1954 to 1997, preserved exactly as it was — the scale of it, the detail in every cabin, and the contrast between the state rooms and the crew quarters.
LunchQuick lunch back near Waverley. Collect bags from the hotel on the way past — it is on the same street.
AfternoonLNER from Edinburgh Waverley to London King’s Cross — about 4hrs 30min. Sit on the east side again for the Northumberland coast in reverse. Book a table seat; it is a long run and a good one.
EveningArrive King’s Cross early evening. Check in at the hotel a few minutes from the station. Dinner locally — keep it simple, the flight is tomorrow.

Visa note — UK Standard Visitor Visa & Short Stay Visa Waiver Programme (SSVWP)
Jenifer will need a UK Standard Visitor Visa before travelling. This itinerary is a key supporting document: it shows a clear route with exact dates (4–17 October 2026), named hotels, and realistic travel connections.

Sponsorship: All costs of the trip are covered by Simon (Jenifer’s partner, Irish citizen). The visa application should include a signed sponsorship letter from Simon, his 3-month bank statements, and payslips. Jenifer should also include her IBM employment letter and her own bank statements (to demonstrate stable employment and ties to the Philippines, not to show self-funding).

Flight tickets: UKVI does not require confirmed/paid flight tickets with the application. A proposed itinerary showing intended dates and routes is sufficient. Do not purchase non-refundable tickets before the visa decision is received. Book flights after the visa is granted.

As a Philippine national, Jenifer also qualifies for Ireland’s Short Stay Visa Waiver Programme (SSVWP) — a valid UK visa permits entry to Ireland for up to 90 days with no separate Irish visa needed. This is distinct from BIVS (China/India only).

Visa application — preparation guide

📄  This guide is for Simon and Jenifer only — not part of the visa submission
Flight tickets — do not book before the visa is granted

UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) does not require confirmed or paid flight tickets as part of the application. A proposed itinerary showing intended dates, airline, and route is sufficient. The Home Office specifically advises against purchasing non-refundable tickets before a decision, as processing delays or a refusal would result in financial loss. Book flights only after the visa stamp is in the passport.

Sponsorship letter — Simon (most important document)

Simon should write a signed letter on plain paper (with full name, address, phone, and email at the top) addressed to the Entry Clearance Officer. It should clearly state: his full name and nationality; his relationship to Jenifer; that he is covering all costs of the trip (flights, accommodation, transport, activities, and day-to-day expenses); an approximate total cost figure; and that he has sufficient funds to cover this without hardship. It should also state the UK departure date of 17 October 2026 and where Jenifer travels next. Only write what is true on the day it is signed — the letter, this itinerary, and anything said at the border must all say the same thing.

Simon’s bank statements

3 months of statements. The caseworker is looking for: a regular salary credit each month (ideally labelled “OOMNITZA” or similar so the employer is identifiable); a healthy average balance comfortably above the total trip cost; and no large unexplained outflows. Include 2–3 recent payslips alongside the statements.

Jenifer’s bank statements

Jenifer should still submit her own 3-month bank statements even though Simon is the sponsor. These are not submitted to show she is self-funding — they are evidence of stable employment and financial ties to the Philippines. The key thing the caseworker wants to see is a regular IBM salary credit arriving each month. The account balance is secondary when sponsorship is clearly documented.

If a minimum balance transfer is made

If you decide to transfer funds into Jenifer’s account to show a minimum balance, the transfer should happen at least 4 weeks before the application is submitted and the funds should remain in the account. A large deposit made immediately before submission can raise suspicion. The transfer must be accompanied by a signed letter from Simon confirming: the amount transferred, that it originates from his personal account, and the reason for the transfer. Include both Simon’s bank statement showing the debit and Jenifer’s statement showing the credit.

Consistency check

The total accommodation, activity, and transport costs in the sponsorship letter should be consistent with what is listed in the Visitor Travel Itinerary below. If a caseworker totals the hotel costs from the itinerary, it should match what the letter claims. The itinerary itself is part of the financial evidence package.
