Day 1 4 Oct Sunday
Arrival & the South Bank
DaytimeJenifer flies out of Manila on 4 Oct and arrives at Heathrow either later the same evening or in the early hours of 5 Oct, depending on the routing booked. Simon arrives from Cork on 4 Oct. Both take the Elizabeth Line directly into central London. Check in, drop bags, get settled.
EveningIf the arrival lands early enough, a slow South Bank walk to stretch the legs — Millennium Bridge, the view of St Paul’s from the south bank, the riverfront at dusk. Dinner at a riverside restaurant. Early night; it is a very long travel day.
Arrival may fall in the early hours of 5 OctElizabeth Line from LHR: ~£14pp
Day 2 5 Oct Monday
Westminster & the Natural History Museum
MorningWestminster walk. Start at Westminster Bridge for the Parliament and Big Ben view. St James’s Park — pelicans, deck chairs, the palace at the far end. Buckingham Palace exterior and Horse Guards Parade. All on foot, no rush.
LunchNorth through Soho — Wardour Street or Greek Street for a relaxed lunch.
AfternoonNatural History Museum, South Kensington (free, 2–2.5hrs). Hintze Hall and the blue whale skeleton, the dinosaur gallery, Earth Hall through the iron globe. A Victorian cathedral built for the natural world — the building is half the reason to go.
EveningPiccadilly Line straight back to Covent Garden for a wander and dinner. Covent Garden and Seven Dials have good restaurants at every price point.
Natural History Museum — freePiccadilly Line: South Ken → Covent Garden, direct
Day 3 6 Oct Tuesday
The Tower & East London
MorningTower Bridge on foot from the hotel along the Thames Path, then the Tower of London (pre-book). The Crown Jewels, the White Tower, the ravens. Join a free Yeoman Warder tour immediately on entry — they are genuinely excellent. Allow 2hrs.
LunchBrick Lane. A salt beef bagel from Beigel Bake at the north end — open 24hrs, a proper London institution, about £5.
AfternoonShoreditch & Spitalfields. Street art around Hanbury Street and Princelet Street, Old Spitalfields Market, and the vintage shops along Brick Lane. This area rewards slow walking with no fixed plan.
EveningDinner in Shoreditch — one of London’s best areas for independent restaurants.
Pre-book Tower of LondonBeigel Bake — north end of Brick Lane, 24hrs
Day 4 7 Oct Wednesday
Greenwich & the Thames by Boat
MorningThames Clipper from Bankside Pier, a 5-minute walk from the hotel, east to Greenwich. The river journey is one of the great London views: the City skyline, Canary Wharf, the O2. Royal Observatory & the Prime Meridian (pre-book) at the top of the park, with panoramic views back over the city.
LunchGreenwich Market — a covered market with a wide range of street food stalls and a good browse afterwards.
AfternoonCutty Sark (pre-book) — the last surviving Victorian tea clipper, raised on a glass base so you walk beneath the hull. Then a slow wander back down through Greenwich Park to the pier.
EveningLast dinner of this London stay. Pack tonight; the York train is tomorrow morning.
Thames Clipper: Bankside → Greenwich ~40minCombined Observatory + Cutty Sark ticket at rmg.co.uk

Day 5 8 Oct Thursday
Arrive York — Medieval Streets
MorningCheck out of the London hotel. Tube to Kings Cross and board the LNER north. ~2hrs to York. Arrive early afternoon.
AfternoonCheck in, then straight to The Shambles — the most famous medieval street in England, so narrow in places the overhanging buildings nearly touch. Walk the surrounding lanes and the snickelways, York’s network of tiny alleyways.
Late PMCity Walls — a free 3km circuit of the Roman and medieval walls. The stretch from Bootham Bar to Monk Bar has the best views over the Minster. Allow 1–1.5hrs.
EveningDinner in York. Gillygate and Micklegate have a good range of independent restaurants.
The Shambles — freeCity Walls walk — freeBook advance train: £20–40pp
Day 6 9 Oct Friday
York Minster, Betty’s & the Spa
MorningYork Minster and the tower climb (pre-book) — the best vantage point in the city. The cathedral is one of the finest Gothic buildings in Northern Europe; allow 1.5hrs inside.
Late AMNational Railway Museum (free, 10-min walk). The Flying Scotsman, a Japanese bullet train, the royal carriages. More engaging than it sounds, and it is on the way to the spa.
LunchBetty’s Tea Room on St Helen’s Square. Expect a 15–20 minute queue that moves steadily. A Yorkshire institution in the same building since 1936.
AfternoonThe Spa at The Grand, beside York station (day access, no hotel stay needed). Hydrotherapy pool, thermal suite, treatments. Book both treatments at the same time so the session overlaps. The best-earned afternoon of the first week.
Pre-book York Minster tower climbBook The Grand spa 2–3 weeks aheadNational Railway Museum — free
Day 7 10 Oct Saturday
Jorvik & the Museum Gardens
MorningJorvik Viking Centre (pre-book). Below the modern street lies the excavated site of a Viking-age neighbourhood, explored by a slow-moving car through a reconstruction of 10th-century Jorvik. Built on the actual dig site. Allow 1.5hrs.
LunchRelaxed lunch in the city — Goodramgate or Gillygate for independent restaurants and cafes.
AfternoonMuseum Gardens (free) — the open-air ruins of St Mary’s Abbey in a botanic garden by the river, a quiet spot most visitors miss. Then Clifford’s Tower for the view, and a slow walk along the River Ouse.
EveningFinal York dinner. Pack tonight — the Edinburgh train is tomorrow morning.
Pre-book Jorvik Viking CentreMuseum Gardens & Abbey ruins — free

Day 8 11 Oct Sunday
Arrive Edinburgh — First Evening on the Royal Mile
MorningCheck out of York and take the train north. ~2hrs 30min, arriving Edinburgh Waverley early afternoon. The hotel is a 10-minute walk up the Royal Mile from the station.
AfternoonCheck in, drop bags. First walk along the Royal Mile — explore the closes, the narrow alleyways branching off either side. Each one leads somewhere unexpected: a hidden courtyard, a staircase, a view. No plan needed.
EveningDinner in the Grassmarket — a lively square at the foot of the castle rock, surrounded by restaurants. A good introduction to the Old Town.
York → Edinburgh Waverley: ~2hrs 30minHotel: 10-min walk from Waverley
Day 9 12 Oct Monday
Edinburgh Castle & the Old Town
MorningEdinburgh Castle (pre-book, arrive at opening). The Crown Jewels, the Stone of Destiny, the One O’Clock Gun. The views from the ramparts over the city, Arthur’s Seat, and the Firth of Forth are extraordinary. Allow 2.5–3hrs.
LunchDown the Royal Mile to one of the older pubs just off the Mile — The Jolly Judge, down a close near the castle, is quiet and away from the main flow.
AfternoonOld Town wander. St Giles’ Cathedral (free — the Thistle Chapel inside is extraordinary). Victoria Street, the curved colourful street said to have inspired Diagon Alley. Greyfriars Kirkyard and Greyfriars Bobby. A neighbourhood to explore slowly rather than tick off.
EveningCalton Hill at sunset. A 5-minute climb from the east end of Princes Street to 360° views over the whole city. One of Edinburgh’s best free things. October light on a clear evening is remarkable.
Pre-book Edinburgh Castle: sells out in OctoberSt Giles’ Cathedral — freeCalton Hill — free
Day 10 13 Oct Tuesday
Arthur’s Seat & One Spa
Early AMArthur’s Seat. Start by 8am. An ancient volcano in the middle of the city, 251m up, about 45 minutes to the summit on the main path from Holyrood. The views over Edinburgh, the Firth of Forth, and the Pentlands are the finest in the city. Bring a layer; it is always windy at the top.
Late AMWalk back down past Holyrood Palace and the free-to-view Abbey ruins. Coffee at the foot of the Royal Mile, well earned.
AfternoonOne Spa at the Sheraton Grand, Festival Square (day access, no hotel stay needed). The rooftop hydropool looks straight at the castle, with a full thermal suite below it. Book 2–3 weeks ahead and book treatments for both at the same time.
EveningPrinces Street Gardens and the New Town on the walk back — the castle view from the gardens is the best in the city. Dinner on George Street.
Arthur’s Seat — freeBook One Spa 2–3 weeks aheadGood walking shoes for the hike
Day 11 14 Oct Wednesday
Leith, the Royal Yacht & Dean Village
MorningBus 16 or the tram down to Leith, about 20 minutes. Royal Yacht Britannia (pre-book the 10am slot, allow 2hrs). The royal family’s private floating home from 1954 to 1997, preserved exactly as it was — the state rooms, the tiny private cabins, the engine room.
LunchBack to Stockbridge, Edinburgh’s most pleasant neighbourhood. St Stephen Street and the high street have a strong run of independent cafes and bistros.
AfternoonWater of Leith Walkway to Dean Village (free). The path drops into a hidden gorge below the New Town and the city disappears entirely. St Bernard’s Well on the way, and Dean Village itself is a former mill village most visitors never find. About 45 minutes at an easy pace.
EveningLast dinner in Scotland — book somewhere worth the occasion. Pack tonight; the London train is tomorrow.
Pre-book Britannia, 10am slotWater of Leith & Dean Village — freeWet weather: swap in the National Museum of Scotland

Windsor Castle
MorningTube to Paddington, then Great Western Railway to Windsor & Eton Central, changing at Slough. About 55 minutes door to door, and the station sits at the bottom of the castle wall. Windsor Castle (pre-book the 10am slot) — the oldest and largest occupied castle in the world. St George’s Chapel and the State Apartments. Allow 2.5–3hrs.

Visa application — preparation guide

📄  This guide is for Simon and Jenifer only — not part of the visa submission
Flight tickets — do not book before the visa is granted

UK Visas and Immigration (UKVI) does not require confirmed or paid flight tickets as part of the application. A proposed itinerary showing intended dates, airline, and route is sufficient. The Home Office specifically advises against purchasing non-refundable tickets before a decision, as processing delays or a refusal would result in financial loss. Book flights only after the visa stamp is in the passport.

Sponsorship letter — Simon (most important document)

Simon should write a signed letter on plain paper (with full name, address, phone, and email at the top) addressed to the Entry Clearance Officer. It should clearly state: his full name and nationality; his relationship to Jenifer; that he is covering all costs of the trip (flights, accommodation, transport, activities, and day-to-day expenses); an approximate total cost figure; and that he has sufficient funds to cover this without hardship. It should also state the UK departure date of 17 October 2026 and where Jenifer travels next. Only write what is true on the day it is signed — the letter, this itinerary, and anything said at the border must all say the same thing.

Simon’s bank statements

3 months of statements. The caseworker is looking for: a regular salary credit each month (ideally labelled “OOMNITZA” or similar so the employer is identifiable); a healthy average balance comfortably above the total trip cost; and no large unexplained outflows. Include 2–3 recent payslips alongside the statements.

Jenifer’s bank statements

Jenifer should still submit her own 3-month bank statements even though Simon is the sponsor. These are not submitted to show she is self-funding — they are evidence of stable employment and financial ties to the Philippines. The key thing the caseworker wants to see is a regular IBM salary credit arriving each month. The account balance is secondary when sponsorship is clearly documented.

If a minimum balance transfer is made

If you decide to transfer funds into Jenifer’s account to show a minimum balance, the transfer should happen at least 4 weeks before the application is submitted and the funds should remain in the account. A large deposit made immediately before submission can raise suspicion. The transfer must be accompanied by a signed letter from Simon confirming: the amount transferred, that it originates from his personal account, and the reason for the transfer. Include both Simon’s bank statement showing the debit and Jenifer’s statement showing the credit.

Consistency check

The total accommodation, activity, and transport costs in the sponsorship letter should be consistent with what is listed in the Visitor Travel Itinerary below. If a caseworker totals the hotel costs from the itinerary, it should match what the letter claims. The itinerary itself is part of the financial evidence package.
