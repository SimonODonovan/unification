Why York, and why only two nights. York is the right size for the middle of a fortnight. Everything below sits inside a two-mile wall, nothing needs a bus, and the whole place can be walked end to end in about twenty minutes — which, after eight days of the Tube, is the point. Two nights buys one full day plus two half days, and for a first visit that is genuinely enough. It also breaks a five-hour run to Edinburgh into two comfortable ones. English half term does not start until the end of October, so unlike Edinburgh this week, York should be calm.
Day 9
North to York
Monday, 12 October 2026  ·  A travel day, and then a very old street
Nothing before noon, and one thing with a time on it. Southwark checkout is midday, so there is no reason to be at King’s Cross at eight in the morning — you earned a slow start with the East End on Sunday. The only fixed point today is evensong at the Minster at about 5:15pm, and even that is free to walk away from. Everything else is a wander.

Afternoon — do nothing organised
🏛  Medieval street, free
The Shambles & the snickelways
📍  The Shambles, York YO1 7LZ  ·  5-minute walk from the Minster
Free 🕐  Open street, any time ⏱  An hour, or three

Go this afternoon, before you have read anything about it. The Shambles is a street of overhanging timber-framed butchers’ shops that has barely changed since the 1400s — the upper storeys lean so far in that neighbours could shake hands across the top. It is in the Domesday Book. It is also, unavoidably, one of the most photographed streets in England, so the trick is when you go, not whether: it is heaving between eleven and four, and it belongs to almost nobody after about five o’clock or before nine in the morning.

  • Shambles Market is directly behind it, through any of the gaps — an open-air market of around eighty stalls with a covered street-food row. That is tomorrow’s lunch, but have a look now.
  • The snickelways are York’s named alleys — Mad Alice Lane, Pope’s Head Alley, Coffee Yard, Hornpot Lane. There are dozens, they mostly connect streets you are already on, and getting lost in them for an hour is the best free thing to do in York.
  • The York Ghost Merchants, on the Shambles itself, sells small handmade plaster ghosts in a beautifully theatrical shop. The queue can run an hour. Worth it if it is short, absolutely not if it is long.
  • Stonegate, running from the Minster, is the better street for shops and has the Roman road underneath it. Look up for the red devil on the corner of Coffee Yard.

About 5:15pm — the one thing with a time on it
⛪  Sung service, free, no ticket
Evensong at York Minster
📍  Deangate, York YO1 7HH  ·  Enter by the south door and say you are there for evensong
Free  no booking 🕐  Usually 5:15pm ⏱  About 45 minutes

If you do one thing today, make it this. You are let in free, you sit in the quire — the carved wooden enclosure at the heart of the building, which you would otherwise only see roped off — and a choir sings for three quarters of an hour while the light goes out of the windows above you. Nobody expects anything of you, nobody minds that you are not religious, and you come out having been inside the best building in the north of England at the best hour of its day, for nothing. It is also, quietly, the gentlest possible landing after a travel day.

Check the service list for that week before counting on it. Evensong is typically 5:15pm Tuesday to Saturday in term time, but Monday is often said Evening Prayer rather than sung, and choirs take holidays. The Minster publishes the coming week’s services on its own site. If Monday is said rather than sung, go anyway — it is shorter and you still get the quire — or move it to Tuesday at 5:15 and swap tomorrow’s dusk walk.
Arrive by about 5:05. Tell the steward at the door you are there for evensong and you will be walked past the ticket desk. Coats on: the Minster in October is genuinely cold.

👻  Optional, after dinner If you are up for it
A ghost walk
📍  Most walks leave from the Minster gates or the Shambles  ·  around 7:30pm, nightly
💰  About £8–10 each, cash on the night ⏱  About 75 minutes 🕐  Most need no booking

York calls itself the most haunted city in Europe and has an entire cottage industry to prove it. Several walks leave around half seven every night, they cost about a tenner, you turn up rather than book, and in the second week of October the dark and the damp do most of the work. It is a cheap, low-commitment way to see the snickelways lit at night with someone telling you why each one is awful. The Ghost Trail of York and The Bloody Tour of York are the two usually named first — check current departure points on the day, since they move.

Skip it without guilt. You will have been on a train and on your feet all afternoon. This is here because it is genuinely fun and genuinely optional, not because the day needs it.

Day 10
All of York, on Foot
Tuesday, 13 October 2026  ·  The only full day — and no transport at all
The shape of the day. It runs outward from the Minster and loops back: cathedral first thing while it is empty, straight up onto the walls from the door, market lunch, one museum in the afternoon, then the Museum Gardens as the light goes. Total walking is about four miles, all flat, all inside the walls, and you pass the hotel twice — so anything can be dropped at any point without wrecking what follows. Two things need booking in advance: the tower climb and dinner.
9:30am, at opening — before the coaches
⛪  Cathedral, ticketed
York Minster
📍  Deangate, York YO1 7HH  ·  2 minutes from Dean Court
💰  About £20 each — ticket valid 12 months 🕐  Sightseeing from 9:30am, Mon–Sat ⏱  90 minutes, plus the tower

The largest Gothic cathedral in northern Europe, and it earns the superlative. Two things in particular: the Great East Window, roughly the size of a tennis court and the largest single expanse of medieval stained glass in the world, and the Chapter House, an octagonal room with no central pillar holding up its roof — which in the 1280s was showing off. Go at 9:30 sharp. The first hour is the whole difference between a cathedral and a crowd.

  • The Central Tower climb — 275 steps up a spiral staircase to the roof, and the only place you see every roof in York at once with the Vale of York behind. Booked separately, timed, and it sells out. Take the earliest slot of the day. Not for anyone uneasy on tight spiral stairs: it is genuinely narrow and there is no way back past people coming up.
  • The Undercroft is included and often skipped — the Roman fortress and Norman cathedral beneath the current building, plus the Minster’s treasures. Twenty minutes, and it is where the whole building starts making sense.
  • The Five Sisters Window in the north transept: five lancets of 13th-century grisaille glass, grey-green and almost colourless, and quietly the most beautiful thing in the building.
  • Your ticket lasts a year and covers re-entry, so there is no reason to rush the morning.
Book the tower with the ticket, now. The climb is a separate timed add-on of roughly £10 and it is the one thing here that regularly sells out. Minimum age 8, no bags, and it closes in high wind — if it is blowing on the day they refund it.

Late morning — straight up onto the wall
🏰  City walls, free
The City Walls — Bootham Bar to Monk Bar
📍  Bootham Bar, High Petergate, York YO1 7EN
Free 🕐  8am to dusk — they lock the gates ⏱  15 min for the good bit, 2 hrs for all of it

York has the most complete medieval city walls in England — about two miles of them, raised on an earth rampart, and you walk along the top. The Bootham Bar to Monk Bar stretch is the best half-mile of wall in the country: it runs along the back of the Minster with the whole east front and both towers rising over the gardens to your right, and it takes about fifteen minutes. If you do nothing else on the walls, do that.

  • The full circuit is about two miles and takes a gentle two hours with stops. It is not continuous — you drop to street level four or five times where the wall is gone — and the signage handles it for you.
  • Monk Bar is the tallest of the gates and still has a working portcullis. There is a small Richard III museum inside it if you want one.
  • Micklegate Bar, on the far side, is the gate where they used to put the heads. Traditionally the one monarchs enter by.
  • The southern stretch, Fishergate to Baile Hill, is the quiet one with nobody on it — worth knowing if you decide to walk the whole loop.
They genuinely lock it at dusk. The walls close at sunset — about 6:15pm in mid-October — and staff walk the circuit clearing people off beforehand. Fine today; just do not plan to still be up there at seven.

🍱  Market & street food
Shambles Market
📍  Shambles Market, York YO1 7PW  ·  5 minutes down from Monk Bar
Free  to wander 🕐  Daily, roughly 9am to 5pm 💰  £6–10 a plate

Come off the wall at Monk Bar, down Goodramgate, and into the market from behind the Shambles. Around eighty stalls in the open air, with a permanent covered street-food row down one side — a dozen kitchens, everything from Thai to Yorkshire pudding wraps, all under a tenner. Eat standing up, eat two things, do not commit to a restaurant. There is a proper dinner tonight and a museum in between.

Sitting down instead: Mannion & Co on Blake Street (superb deli-cafe, no bookings, expect a short wait) or the Guy Fawkes Inn on High Petergate, in the house he was born in, three minutes from the Minster.

Afternoon — pick one, not all three
The recommendation
York Castle Museum + Clifford’s Tower

A whole Victorian street rebuilt indoors, and a Norman keep across the road. The odder, warmer, more charming afternoon, and the two are sixty seconds apart. About £16 + £9.

If you want the big free one
National Railway Museum

Free, enormous, and the Mallard and a bullet train are in it. Behind the station, so it is fifteen minutes back the wrong way. Check what is open — it has been mid-redevelopment.

If you want the York-only one
JORVIK Viking Centre

A slow ride through a reconstructed Viking street built over the real dig, smell and all. Faintly ridiculous, genuinely good. About £15, timed, book ahead.

🏛  Museum, ticketed  ·  the pick
York Castle Museum & Clifford’s Tower
📍  Eye of York, York YO1 9RY  ·  10 minutes from the Shambles, down Coppergate
💰  About £16 (museum) + £9 (tower) 🕐  Roughly 10am to 5pm ⏱  2 hours for both

The reason to come is Kirkgate: a full Victorian street reconstructed inside the museum — real shopfronts, cobbles, a hansom cab, gas lamps, and the lighting cycling from day to night while you stand in it. It is the best thing of its kind in the country, and genuinely transporting rather than worthy. The rest is social history done well — the sixties gallery, the debtors’ prison in the old cells, and the condemned cell where Dick Turpin spent his last night.

  • Clifford’s Tower is sixty seconds away across the grass — a 13th-century quatrefoil keep on a Norman motte. It reopened in 2022 with a new internal deck and roof walkway, so you climb through the middle of the shell and come out on top with a view right round the city. Half an hour, and a separate English Heritage ticket.
  • The mound is steep and the tower has a lot of steps. If only one of the two appeals, the museum is the better hour.
  • Both are ten minutes from the hotel, so this is the point in the day where you can bail out and go back for a bath instead. No shame in it — you walked York’s walls this morning.

Late afternoon, about 4:30 — sit down
🌳  Botanic gardens & abbey ruins, free
Museum Gardens & St Mary’s Abbey
📍  Museum Street, York YO1 7FR  ·  5 minutes from the Minster
Free 🕐  Open until dusk ⏱  45 minutes, mostly sitting

Ten acres of botanical garden in the middle of the city, with the ruined nave of St Mary’s Abbey standing in the grass in the middle of it — a Benedictine house dissolved in 1539, now a row of roofless Gothic arches with squirrels in them. Free, quiet, and at its best in the second week of October, when the light comes in low and gold through the trees at about half four. There are benches. Use them: this is the one deliberately unproductive hour of the day, and the day is built around having it.

  • The Multangular Tower, in the corner of the gardens, is Roman — the west corner of the fortress of the Ninth Legion, with the medieval wall built straight on top of it. You can see the join.
  • The Hospitium and the abbey precinct wall run down to the river. Come out of the bottom gate onto the Ouse and walk back up over Lendal Bridge.
  • The Yorkshire Museum sits in the middle of the gardens if the weather turns — Roman and Viking York, the Middleham Jewel, about £9.

🍸  Dinner — the York dinner, and the one to book
Skosh, 98 Micklegate. The recommendation. Small plates built on Yorkshire produce with a heavy hand of Asian and Middle Eastern technique — the most interesting cooking in York by a distance, and about £45–55 a head for a proper spread. It is a small room and it books out weeks ahead. Reserve now, not in October. Fifteen minutes from the Minster, over Ouse Bridge.
Los Moros, 13 Grape Lane. Moroccan and North African, started as a market stall, now a proper restaurant in a timbered building off Stonegate. Warmer and cheaper than Skosh, about £30 a head, five minutes from the hotel.
Le Cochon Aveugle, 37 Walmgate. A blind tasting menu — you are not told what is coming. Superb, and the biggest commitment of the four: three hours and about £90 a head.
Roots, 68 Marygate. Tommy Banks’s Michelin-starred place, five minutes from Dean Court. The splurge, if York rather than Edinburgh is where the money goes.

The Blue Bell
📍  53 Fossgate, York YO1 9TF
💰  Pub prices ⏱  One drink 🕐  Closes around 11pm

The smallest pub in York and the only one with its 1903 interior completely intact — two tiny rooms, panelling, a serving hatch, and a house rule against phones on the tables. It holds about thirty people and it is listed for the inside rather than the outside. If it is full, and it often is, Ye Olde Starre Inne off Stonegate has been trading since 1644 and has a yard.

A Slow Morning, then the Best Train Ride in Britain
Wednesday, 14 October 2026  ·  York to Edinburgh
The train is the 12:19, and that was deliberate. The morning here is worth more than an extra hour in Edinburgh, and the middle-of-the-day train is the one you want anyway — you cross the Northumberland coast in good light rather than staring at your own reflection. Into Waverley at 14:39. Bags stay at the hotel after checkout; every York hotel will hold them.
Morning — one last hour, unhurried
☕  Tea rooms, an institution
Bettys — and the trick for avoiding the queue
📍  Little Bettys, 46 Stonegate, York YO1 8AS  ·  2 minutes from the Minster
💰  About £12–20 each 🕐  From about 9am ⏱  An hour

Bettys has been on St Helen’s Square since 1936, and the queue down the pavement is part of the furniture — it takes no bookings for the main tea rooms, and by half ten on a weekday it can be forty minutes. The trick: go to Little Bettys instead. Same company, same kitchen, same fat rascals, in a much smaller two-storey shop on Stonegate, two minutes from the Minster — and the queue is routinely a fraction of the size, because almost everybody walks straight past it to join the one on the square. Go at nine, and sit upstairs.

If both queues are absurd — and on a wet Wednesday they will not be — Mannion & Co on Blake Street does breakfast better anyway, and Brew & Brownie on Museum Street does the best brunch in York.

🏛  Guildhall, ticketed If there is an hour left
Merchant Adventurers’ Hall
📍  Fossgate, York YO1 9XD  ·  8 minutes from the Minster
💰  About £8 🕐  From 10am ⏱  40 minutes

The best building in York that nobody queues for. A timber-framed guildhall built in 1357 and still owned by the guild that built it — you walk into a vast oak hall under a crown-post roof, then down into the undercroft hospital and the chapel. It is almost always nearly empty, it is eight quid, and it is a better use of a last York hour than another lap of the Shambles. It is also indoors and on the way to nothing, which makes it the ideal wet-morning fallback.

Around midday — north
🚂  LNER, direct
York → Edinburgh Waverley
📍  York station, Station Road, York YO24 1AB  ·  10 minutes on foot from the Minster
⏱  About 2hr 30min, no changes 💰  Booked — 12:19, Coach G 🕐  Roughly hourly

Here is the tip that actually matters on this trip: sit on the right-hand side going north. Not the left. For about forty minutes north of Newcastle the East Coast Main Line runs along the very top of the Northumberland cliffs, close enough that the sea fills the window — and all of it is on the right.

  • Durham, about 45 minutes in — the train crosses a high viaduct and the cathedral and castle stand up on their rock across the river to the right. It lasts about eight seconds. Have the camera out from Darlington.
  • Alnmouth, then the long coastal run: dunes, empty beach, and Bamburgh Castle out on its crag to the right about ten minutes north of Alnmouth.
  • Lindisfarne and its causeway, tide permitting, then Berwick-upon-Tweed, where you cross the Royal Border Bridge — a stone viaduct high above the Tweed estuary with the town spread out below.
  • Then Scotland: Burnmouth, St Abbs, Torness, and the sea all the way to Dunbar. You get the whole Northumberland coast for the price of a seat reservation.
Booked: the 12:19, into Waverley at 14:39. Coach G, seats 13 and 14, reference AV764744 — two Advance singles at £34.80 each. One thing still to check: the tickets do not say which side of the coach those seats are on, and the right-hand side going north is the entire point of this leg. Look it up in the LNER app or on the seat map before the day. If they are on the left, no drama — walk to the vestibule around Alnmouth and stand at the door window. Nobody minds.

Swap-ins and wet weather

Two nights leaves one full afternoon of genuine choice, and October in Yorkshire will make some of that choice for you. Nothing below changes the shape of the days — each is a like-for-like replacement for a slot already in the plan.

Appendix A
Swap-ins for Tuesday Afternoon
All within ten minutes of the Shambles
🚆  Museum, free
National Railway Museum
📍  Leeman Road, York YO26 4XJ  ·  5 minutes behind the station
Free  donation suggested ⏱  2 to 3 hours

Enormous, free, and home to Mallard — the fastest steam locomotive ever built — a Japanese bullet train, and the Royal Trains. If either of you likes machinery this beats the Castle Museum outright, and it costs nothing.

Two caveats. It has been in the middle of a long redevelopment, so check which halls are open before committing an afternoon. And it sits behind the station, on the opposite side of the city from everything else on Tuesday — taking it means giving up the walls-and-gardens loop rather than adding to it. It slots much more naturally into Wednesday morning, on the way to the train.

⚔  Immersive museum, ticketed
JORVIK Viking Centre
📍  Coppergate, York YO1 9WT  ·  5 minutes from the Shambles
💰  About £15 🕐  Timed entry — book ⏱  An hour

Built directly over the Coppergate dig, where they found ten metres of preserved Viking-age York. You sit in a small car and are carried through a reconstruction of the street as it stood around 960 — animatronic residents, the correct dialect of Old Norse, and a smell budget that has become famous. It should be terrible and it is not. The genuine excavated finds are in the gallery afterwards.

Best paired with the Castle Museum rather than instead of it — they are seven minutes apart and JORVIK is only an hour, so on a day where you skip the walls circuit you can do both.

🏦  Georgian townhouse, ticketed
Fairfax House
📍  Castlegate, York YO1 9RN  ·  Next door to Clifford’s Tower
💰  About £9 ⏱  An hour

Widely called the finest Georgian townhouse in England, and almost nobody goes. Plasterwork ceilings, a staircase worth the ticket on its own, and a collection of 18th-century furniture standing in the rooms it was made for. Small, warm, quiet, and ninety seconds from the Castle Museum — the natural add-on if the afternoon runs short rather than long.

Appendix B
If It Rains All Day
Tuesday, rebuilt for weather — and it might well be
The only part of Tuesday that rain genuinely ruins is the walls. They are exposed, the stone goes slick, and the view of the Minster — the entire reason to be up there — disappears into the murk. Everything else on the day is either indoors or takes twenty minutes. So the wet version is not a different day; it is the same day with the wall walk cut and one more roof added.
9:30am
Minster, unchanged

Give it two and a half hours instead of ninety minutes — the Undercroft and the Chapter House are exactly where you want to be in the rain. The tower closes in high wind, and they refund it, so check before setting out.

Late morning
Merchant Adventurers’ Hall

Move it forward from Wednesday. Eight minutes from the Minster, forty minutes inside a 1357 oak hall, and almost nobody in it.

Lunch
The covered market row

Shambles Market’s street-food row sits under a permanent roof. Or sit down properly at Mannion & Co on Blake Street.

Afternoon
Castle Museum + JORVIK

Do both rather than choosing — they are seven minutes apart and you have the wall walk’s two hours back. Kirkgate is the best indoor hour in York.

Late afternoon
Yorkshire Museum

Instead of sitting in the Museum Gardens, sit in the museum in the middle of them. Roman and Viking York, about £9, and you still walk out through the abbey ruins.

Evening
Unchanged

Skosh does not care about the weather, and a ghost walk in driving rain is arguably the correct experience. The walls at dusk is the only thing you have actually lost.

Do not write the walls off at breakfast. Yorkshire weather in October moves fast, and the Bootham–Monk stretch is only fifteen minutes long. If it clears at any point on Tuesday — even at half four — you are never more than ten minutes from a staircase up onto it. Keep it as an opportunistic thing rather than a scheduled one.
