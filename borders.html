Five rules that matter more than any answer below

  1. Say what is true on the day. Not what was true in July. Officers spot discrepancies between the story, the ticket and the documents — that is what causes refusals, not a clumsy answer.
  2. Nothing in the July application was false. You were employed, you were planning a fortnight in London and Edinburgh, you meant to go home to your job. All true when written. Plans changed afterwards. That is a change of circumstances, not a misrepresentation, and there is nothing to be defensive about.
  3. Do not memorise a script. Know the real plan — dates, addresses, who is paying, what you are going back to. Recited answers sound rehearsed and invite harder questions.
  4. Answer what is asked, in the order it is asked, then stop. You are not withholding anything by not delivering a three-month itinerary in reply to “how long are you here for?”
  5. Two borders, two asks. At Heathrow you are asking for eleven days in the UK. That is an easy case. The long stay is an Irish question, asked at Dublin.

Pack one

Heathrow Terminal 4

Monday 5 October · 06:25 · QR011 from Doha

Arriving alone. Simon waits in arrivals and cannot come through.

What you are asking for: entry as a visitor from 5 to 16 October, then onward to Ireland. A Philippine passport is not eGate-eligible, so you will see an officer in person — that is normal. Allow 60 to 90 minutes from the aircraft door to arrivals.

The strongest thing you own is your receipts

The Tower of London, St Paul’s, £117 of Warner Bros. Studio Tour tickets, £270 of stalls seats for Totoro, and eight prepaid nights on Borough High Street. Timed, paid, non-refundable.

Nobody buys that as camouflage. It is unarguable proof that the UK visit is genuine and not a stepping stone to somewhere else. Print the confirmations and keep them near the top of the folder.

Documents — printed, in one folder

  • Passport — the exact one the eVisa is linked to. No other passport.
  • UKVI share code, generated in the last few days, printed. Plus the eVisa decision letter.
  • The 2025 Irish visa and entry stamp — tab the page. Strongest single document you carry, and it matters at both borders.
  • Paid UK bookings — Studio Tour, Totoro, the Tower, St Paul’s, Premier Inn.
  • A copy of the July application letter, so what you say now is consistent with what was written then.
  • Boarding pass for QR011, plus the onward flight to Dublin on 16 October.
  • The January flight home, confirmed and changeable.
  • Premier Inn, 135 Borough High Street SE1 1NP — prepaid by Simon, £1,340, 8 nights. Plus [second-week accommodation, 12–16 Oct].
  • Simon’s letter: who he is, the relationship since November 2022, that he is sponsoring and hosting, his address at Knocknalyre, Blarney, Co. Cork T23 T635, and his phone number. Signed, with a copy of his passport photo page.
  • Sponsorship evidence — his bank statements, and her own savings.
  • Relationship evidence — photographs across 2022 to 2026, and the Cebu, Manila and Ireland trips.
  • Evidence about the guardians: shared address, and the support provided.
  • Travel insurance covering the whole period, not a two-week policy.
  • Simon’s phone number written on paper. Phones are put away in the hall.

Do this before leaving the desk

Ask for an ink stamp. The UK border has gone digital and standard visitors are often not stamped, but an officer can stamp on request. This is what Dublin will ask for as proof of prior UK entry.

“I’ll be travelling on to Ireland during this trip and I need evidence of my UK entry. Could you stamp my passport, please?”

If they decline, that is fine — keep every boarding pass and the hotel receipt instead. Do not argue about it.

What they will ask

What is the purpose of your visit?

A holiday with your partner. One sentence.

How long are you staying in the UK?

Until the 16th, then flying to Dublin. This is the answer that makes the conversation easy. The onward ticket proves it.

And after Ireland?

Home in January — give the date. If the flight leaves from Heathrow, say so plainly: you come back through to catch it. If it leaves Dublin, simpler still.

Where are you staying?

The Premier Inn on Borough High Street for the first eight nights, booked and paid by Simon. Then [second week].

Who is paying for this trip?

Simon, as sponsor — the same arrangement declared in the application. Say it plainly; being sponsored is normal for a visitor.

What do you do for work?

Whatever is true on 5 October. If the redundancy has not taken effect: employed at IBM Business Services, on leave. If it has: say so, and that this trip comes before looking for work. Do not hide it. Then move to what you are going back to — the guardians, the home, the savings.

Have you travelled before?

Yes — Ireland in 2025, on an Irish visa, home on time. Volunteer this. It is the best thing you can tell any immigration officer.

If you are taken to a side room

This is further examination. It is not a refusal and it happens routinely. You wait, then are interviewed properly, and your bag or phone may be looked at. Stay calm, keep every answer the same as at the desk, and if you do not know something say so rather than guessing. They may telephone Simon — which is why his number is on paper.

Pack two

Cork Airport

Saturday 17 October, arriving about 08:20

In on the 07:00 from Edinburgh with Simon, then straight on to Blarney. This is the harder of the two borders — the long stay is decided here.

What you are asking for: entry under the Short Stay Visa Waiver Programme, for a stay until the January flight. The Border Management Unit has confirmed that either Cork or Dublin may be used.

Confirmed in writing by the Border Management Unit, 2 September 2026

BMU were asked about a Philippine national holding a UK Standard Visitor visa held digitally as an eVisa, arriving from the UK. Their reply, from ICS McDonnell:

“In the circumstances you describe she should be able to avail of the short stay visa waiver programme. She may present at either Cork or Dublin airport and I would carry copies of her airlines tickets and bookings in and out of the UK as proof of travel. Also she may be asked to present her E-visa digitally on her mobile phone.”

They note that BMU is a frontline service and does not provide pre-clearance, so this is not a guarantee of entry. It is still the responsible unit answering on these exact facts. Print the email and carry it.

On choosing Cork, and a Saturday. The original reason for a Friday was staffing — a weekday desk at a bigger airport sees waiver cases more often. Cork is small and Saturday morning is quiet, so the officer may well need to check with a supervisor. Expect that, and do not read it as a bad sign. Be patient, do not fill the silence, and keep this email on top of the folder so the answer is already in the room. If the Edinburgh flight is cancelled, Dublin remains a fully equivalent fallback — BMU named both — reached by an Edinburgh–Dublin flight and the train south.

Ask for a date, not for ninety days

The realistic risk here is not refusal — it is being granted a short period. An officer comfortable with you but cautious about the maximum might stamp thirty days, and the plan collapses in November.

So do not request “ninety days”. Name the departure date and put the ticket on the counter. Arriving on 17 October, ninety days runs to 14 January, so a flight in the first or second week of January sits comfortably inside — you are asking for less than the maximum, which is an easier grant.

Watch the deadline: the programme requires entry to Ireland by 31 October 2026. The 17th clears it by a fortnight. If the UK leg slips, that date does not move.

Documents — on top of the folder, in this order

  • Passport, open at the 2025 Irish visa and stamp. Lead with this. She has held an Irish visa, entered on it, and left on time. Nothing else in the folder is worth as much here.
  • Tickets and bookings in and out of the UK — this is what BMU specifically advised carrying as proof of travel. The Manila to Heathrow booking and the UK to Ireland booking. The Heathrow entry stamp too, if one was given.
  • Her phone, charged, with working data — BMU note she may be asked to present the eVisa digitally. Have the UKVI login ready, and a saved screenshot or PDF as a fallback if the page will not load.
  • UK eVisa and a share code. There is no sticker in the passport and that is correct — the Irish visa service’s own Philippines notice of 29 June 2026 lists “valid UK visa (or E-visa)” as acceptable. Print that notice and bring it.
  • Printed copy of the Irish Immigration Service page on the Short Stay Visa Waiver Programme.
  • The January flight home, confirmed. The evidence of leaving. If it departs Heathrow rather than Dublin, say so plainly — she flies to London a couple of days beforehand.
  • Simon’s letter and passport copy, naming Knocknalyre, Blarney as the address.
  • Sponsorship and funds — his statements and her savings, covering the whole stay.
  • Evidence about the guardians: that she lives with them, and supports them.
  • Travel insurance covering the full period through to January.
  • Relationship evidence, as at Heathrow.

What they will ask

Have you been to Ireland before?

Yes — in 2025, on a visa granted by the Irish Immigration Service, sponsored by Simon, and home on time. Say this early, even if the question arrives in a different shape. It answers the officer’s real concern before they have to ask it.

How long do you want to stay?

Give the January date and say the flight is booked. Hand over the ticket. Definite, with evidence.

Why so long?

To spend Christmas and New Year with your partner, and there is time because you are between jobs. True, and not disqualifying — but it is why the next answer matters most.

What are you going back to?

This is the question the whole thing turns on. The guardians who raised her, whom she lives with and supports. The home and savings in Daet. Have the evidence in hand, not just the words.

Where will you stay?

Simon’s home, Knocknalyre, Blarney, Co. Cork. He is an Irish citizen and he is standing next to her. Let him give the address and confirm the sponsorship.

Who is supporting you?

Simon, plus her own savings. The same arrangement as the 2025 trip that was approved.

What is your permission to be here?

A UK Standard Visitor visa, entered the UK at Heathrow on 5 October, travelling under the Short Stay Visa Waiver Programme. Hand over the printed notice at this point.

Check the stamp before walking away

Make sure the date is legible. Whatever it says is the deadline, and overstaying it would be far more damaging than a shorter trip.

If it is less than asked for, take it without arguing and move the January flight forward to sit inside the granted period. That is the reason to book it on a changeable fare.

At the airline desk in Edinburgh

Check in at the desk rather than online, and have the printed Irish notice out before anyone asks. Gate staff are increasingly familiar with the waiver, but if one says an Irish visa is needed, ask them politely to check with their duty manager or the destination station rather than accepting a refusal. At bag drop around 05:45 there is time to have that conversation calmly — which is part of why the night before is spent at the airport.

Before she flies

  1. Settle the January flight. Ask Qatar in this order: reissue the return to depart Dublin; or add the Dublin–Heathrow sector to the existing ticket as a connection with bags checked through; or keep the Heathrow departure and book a separate hop, flying over a day early. Confirmed and changeable either way — both borders will ask to see it.
  2. Book the second UK week, 12 to 17 October — York, Edinburgh, and the last night by Edinburgh Airport. Nothing is reserved after the Southwark checkout. See the York and Edinburgh pages.
  3. Change Simon’s return leg. It is currently booked London to Cork on 17 October. He needs to be on the Edinburgh to Cork flight with Jenifer on the morning of the 17th — he is part of the evidence at that border, and should be standing there with a matching boarding pass.
  4. Generate a fresh UKVI share code a few days before departure, and print it.
  5. Print everything. Phones go away at the desk and airport wifi fails at the worst moment.
  6. Tab the passport at the 2025 Irish visa page. It is the key document at both borders.
  7. Buy insurance through to January, not a single short trip.
  8. Settle the redundancy question. If the job has ended, take advice on notifying UKVI before travelling — having told them is a stronger position than not.
