✈️  Getting from Heathrow to the hotel

✈️
Jenifer, arriving at Heathrow (LHR) Note on timing: Jenifer flies out of Manila on 4 October. Depending on the routing booked, she lands at Heathrow either late that same evening or in the early hours of 5 October. Either way the room is booked from the night of the 4th, so it is there whenever she gets in. At the airport, follow signs for the Elizabeth Line (also called Crossrail). ⛪  Abbey, pre-book the first slot
Westminster Abbey
📍  20 Deans Yd, London SW1P 3PA  ·  Westminster station (Jubilee, Circle, District)
💲  About £31pp  ·  Book: westminster-abbey.org 🕐  First entry from 9:30am ⏱  1.5 to 2 hours

The coronation church since 1066, and the burial place of Elizabeth I, Newton, Darwin and Dickens. Going at opening puts you well ahead of the coach parties. Poets’ Corner and the Henry VII Lady Chapel are the highlights.

  • The Henry VII Lady Chapel: A fan-vaulted ceiling that is one of the finest in Europe.
  • Poets’ Corner: Memorials and graves of writers from Chaucer onward.
  • The Coronation Chair: Used at every coronation since 1308.
Strategy: Book the very first entry slot online. The audio guide is included and worth using.

🦖  Natural history museum, free timed entry
Natural History Museum
📍  Cromwell Road, London SW7 5BD  ·  South Kensington (Piccadilly, District, Circle)
Free  (book a timed slot) 🕐  10am to 5:50pm, last entry 5:30pm ⏱  About 2.5 hours

A Victorian cathedral built for the natural world, with carved animals worked into every column. The building is half the reason to go. Do it in this order: Hintze Hall and the blue whale skeleton overhead, then the dinosaur gallery, then Earth Hall.

  • Hintze Hall: The 25-metre blue whale skeleton hangs above the entrance hall. Stand at the top of the stairs for the full view.
  • Dinosaurs: The best-known gallery, quieter later in the afternoon.
  • Earth Hall: Ride the escalator up through the middle of a giant iron globe into the geology galleries.
Tip: Book the free timed ticket online to skip any queue. The V&A and the Science Museum are both two minutes away on the same road and both free if the weather turns.

⛪  Cathedral & dome climb, pre-book
St Paul’s Cathedral
📍  St Paul’s Churchyard, London EC4M 8AD  ·  St Paul’s station (Central line)
💲  About £27pp  ·  Book: stpauls.co.uk 🕐  Galleries open 10am on Wednesdays ⏱  About 2 hours

The galleries open at 10am on Wednesdays specifically, which is the reason today is a Wednesday. The ticket includes the full dome climb: 257 steps to the Whispering Gallery, where a whisper against the wall carries 34 metres to the other side, then 528 in total to the Golden Gallery for views that rival the Shard, for free. The crypt holds Nelson, Wellington and Wren.

  • Whispering Gallery: 257 steps up. No photography here.
  • Golden Gallery: 528 steps in total for the best free view in the City.
  • The crypt: Nelson, Wellington, and Christopher Wren, the cathedral’s architect.
Note: The climb is narrow, steep and has no lift. Wear sensible shoes and take it at your own pace.

Late morning, east through the City on foot
🌱  Three small City stops, all free
Postman’s Park, Leadenhall Market & St Dunstan in the East
📍  Between St Paul’s and the Tower  ·  A walking chain east through the City
Free ⏱  About 1.5 hours with the walking

Three easy stops on the way to the Tower, each a few minutes apart. Do them in order as you head east and finish near the river.

  • Postman’s Park: A small park with a Victorian wall of ceramic tiles memorialising ordinary people who died saving others. Ten minutes, and it stays with you.
  • Leadenhall Market: A Victorian glass and ironwork arcade, used as Diagon Alley in the first Harry Potter film.
  • St Dunstan in the East: A church bombed in the Blitz, left as a ruin and taken over by a garden. Free, tiny, nearly empty, and the most romantic corner in the City.

🏛  Royal fortress, pre-book
Tower of London
📍  Tower Hill, London EC3N 4AB  ·  Tower Hill station (Circle, District)
💲  About £35pp  ·  Book: hrp.org.uk 🕐  Last admission 4:30pm in October ⏱  About 2.5 hours

A thousand-year-old royal fortress on the Thames. Join a Yeoman Warder tour the moment you are through the gate: they run every 30 minutes until mid-afternoon, they are free with entry, and they are comfortably the best thing there. Then the Crown Jewels, the White Tower, and the ravens.

  • Yeoman Warder tour: Free with entry, every 30 minutes, and the highlight. Join one on arrival.
  • Crown Jewels: The actual coronation regalia, on a moving walkway.
  • White Tower: The original Norman keep, with the armour collection including Henry VIII’s.
  • The ravens: Six resident birds, larger than most people expect.
Timing: Book a 2pm timed entry. Last admission is 4:30pm in October, so an afternoon slot still gives you the full 2.5 hours.

About 3:00pm, arriving at the right time for the rut
🦌  Deer park, free
Richmond Park
📍  Enter at Richmond Gate, Richmond TW10 5HP
Free 🕐  7am to dusk ⏱  2 to 3 hours

2,500 acres with about 630 red and fallow deer roaming loose. October is the rut, when red stags and fallow bucks roar, bark and clash antlers competing for females. Late afternoon, an hour or two before sunset, is when the deer are most active and the light is best, so you arrive at exactly the right time.

  • Route: In at Richmond Gate, along toward Pen Ponds where the deer congregate, and back. Pembroke Lodge has a cafe if you need one.
Safety, take it seriously: Keep a minimum of 50 metres from the deer at all times. During the rut the stags are pumped with testosterone, weigh up to 200kg and can reach 30mph. Watch from a distance and do not approach for photographs.

Walk across the Heath, which is woodland and rough grassland rather than a manicured park, and does not feel like a city. Aim for Parliament Hill around 5:45pm for sunset. This is the view: not the Shard, not the Eye, but a grassy hill with the whole city laid out below and Londoners walking dogs past you. Free, and the one that people who actually live there recommend.

Coal Drops Yard & Granary Square
📍  Stable St, London N1C 4DQ  ·  10-minute walk behind King’s Cross
Free  to walk ⏱  An easy evening

Victorian coal warehouses directly behind the station, converted into a run of restaurants along the Regent’s Canal. A good last night that requires no travel at all after four and a half hours on a train. Book somewhere.

🆎  Dinner options, all within 10 minutes
Coal Drops Yard, Stable St, N1C 4DQ. A run of good restaurants along the canal, the best option with any energy left.
German Gymnasium, 1 King’s Boulevard, N1C 4BU. A grand restored Victorian gymnasium two minutes from the hotel, a step up in price and worth it for a last night.
Caravan King’s Cross, Granary Square, N1C 4AA. All-day dining in a converted granary, reliable and bookable.
Tonight: pack properly rather than in the morning. Check the flight time and work backwards. Heathrow is about 50 minutes door to terminal on the Piccadilly line, and a long-haul departure wants three hours at the airport. Confirm the terminal and check in online.

🍴  Last morning, walkable from the hotel
Regent’s Canal towpath & Granary Square
📍  Granary Square, London N1C  ·  10-minute walk north of the hotel
Free  to walk 🕐  Cafes open from 8am ⏱  1 to 1.5 hours

Only worth doing if the flight is afternoon or later. A slow breakfast and a short walk on the Regent’s Canal towpath behind the station is a good way to close out the trip without risking the flight.

  • Granary Square: The fountains and the steps down to the canal, coffee at any of the places around the square.
  • If it rains: the British Library is 5 minutes from the hotel and free. The Treasures gallery holds Magna Carta, Beowulf and the Lindisfarne Gospels in one room, and takes 30 minutes.
Bags: Check out by noon and leave bags at the hotel desk rather than dragging them around. Collect on the way to the Tube.
