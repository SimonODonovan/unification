The market itself is shut on Mondays — Borough trades Tuesday to Sunday only, so the stalls under the roof will not be there.   • Bread Ahead has its own bakery unit on Cathedral St and trades independently of the market days — doughnuts on a Monday.
  • The George Inn, Southwark Cathedral and the Golden Hinde are all within three minutes and all open. The market roof is worth walking under empty anyway; it is a Victorian iron structure and you will see it full tomorrow.

The London Eye

The right thing to do on the first afternoon, and the only day it makes sense: half an hour sitting down in a glass capsule, no walking, no stairs, and the entire fortnight laid out underneath her before she has visited any of it. Day 3
The City & the Tower
Tuesday, 6 October 2026  ·  One walk east, no tube all day
The recovery day, and the tidiest day of the week. This is the first full day after a fourteen-hour flight and seven hours of time difference, so it is deliberately the day with the least in it that still feels like a proper day out: a lie-in, Borough Market on the doorstep, then one continuous walk east through the City to the Tower, up the Sky Garden at sunset, and back along the river in the dark. No trains, no tube, no dressing up, two bookings — the Tower at 2pm and a free Sky Garden slot released three weeks out. Everything is inside a twenty-five minute walk of the hotel, so if she flags at any point the day can simply stop and you are home in a quarter of an hour. Dinner is local and casual and you can be in bed by ten.

Tower of London
📍  Tower Hill, London EC3N 4AB  ·  Tower Hill station (Circle, District)
💲  About £35pp  ·  Book: hrp.org.uk 🕐  Last admission 4:30pm in October ⏱  About 2.5 hours

A thousand-year-old royal fortress on the Thames. Join a Yeoman Warder tour the moment you are through the gate: they run every 30 minutes until mid-afternoon, they are free with entry, and they are comfortably the best thing there. Then the Crown Jewels, the White Tower, and the ravens.

  • Yeoman Warder tour: Free with entry, every 30 minutes, and the highlight. Join one on arrival.
  • Crown Jewels: The actual coronation regalia, on a moving walkway.
  • White Tower: The original Norman keep, with the armour collection including Henry VIII’s.
  • The ravens: Six resident birds, larger than most people expect.
Timing: Book a 2pm timed entry. Last admission is 4:30pm in October, so an afternoon slot still gives you the full 2.5 hours.

Sky Garden, the Walkie Talkie
📍  1 Sky Garden Walk, 20 Fenchurch St, London EC3M 8AF  ·  Two minutes from the Garden at 120
Free  (timed slot, book at skygarden.london) 🕐  Free slots release about 3 weeks ahead and go fast ⏱  1 hour

Three storeys of terraced planting under a curved glass roof on the 35th floor, with an open-air terrace on the south side and the river straight below it. It is the highest thing in London you can get into for nothing, and on this day it is two minutes from where you are already standing — so the walk home along the Thames Path starts from up here instead of from street level. Aim for a slot around sunset: the Shard and Tower Bridge light up on one side, St Paul’s and the City on the other.

  • Book the moment the slots open. They are released roughly three weeks ahead on skygarden.london and the sunset slots are the first to go. Put it in the checklist as a diary reminder rather than a task you can do today.
  • Photo ID and airport-style security at the entrance on Philpot Lane. Allow ten minutes to get up, and no large bags.
  • If the free slots are gone, book a table at the Darwin Brasserie or one of the bars up there instead, which gets you in without a visitor ticket — or just use the Garden at 120 above, which needs nothing at all.
  • The terrace is genuinely outdoors and 155 metres up in October. Bring the coat you were going to walk home in anyway.

St Paul’s Cathedral
📍  St Paul’s Churchyard, London EC4M 8AD  ·  St Paul’s station (Central line)
💲  About £27pp  ·  Book: stpauls.co.uk 🕐  Galleries open 10am on Wednesdays ⏱  About 2 hours

The galleries open at 10am on Wednesdays specifically, which is the reason today is a Wednesday. The ticket includes the full dome climb: 257 steps to the Whispering Gallery, where a whisper against the wall carries 34 metres to the other side, then 528 in total to the Golden Gallery for views that rival the Shard, for free. 🍺  The Marquis Cornwallis — booked, 1:00pm to 2:30pm
This is the main meal of the day, not a refuel. The 16:30 entry buys you a proper sit-down lunch instead of eating standing up outside a station. After this there is only the Backlot Café midway round the tour, and dinner will be late and small, so eat like it is dinner.
Booked: The Marquis Cornwallis, 31 Marchmont St, WC1N 1AP, 020 7278 8355. A table for two, 1:00pm to 2:30pm. Russell Square station is at the bottom of Marchmont Street, two minutes away, and the pub sits north of Bloomsbury on the Euston side, which is the real reason it won the day: it takes about four minutes off the walk to the train.
The table is yours for ninety minutes and no longer, which the confirmation states plainly — and they reserve the right to give it away if you are more than fifteen minutes late. So leave Postman’s Park at 12:25, not later: it is a solid half hour on foot and there is no margin in it. Set an alarm for the park, not for the pub.
The 2:30 finish helps rather than hurts. Twelve minutes to Euston puts you on the concourse about quarter to three for a train out by three, which leaves slack in hand. Order early rather than grazing — ninety minutes is a proper sit-down lunch, but it is not a lingering one, so get the food in rather than easing into it.
The food is ballast, which is what this day needs: slow-cooked steak and pale ale pie with mash and buttered leeks £20.50, beer-battered Atlantic haddock and chips £20.50, outdoor-bred Cumberland sausage and mash £17.50, chicken schnitzel £19.50, an 8oz sirloin at £31.50 if you are going for it. The snacks are the best value here: any three for £18.50 — fried chicken with chilli honey, beef shin croquettes, a handmade scotch egg, sticky pork ribs, halloumi fries. Sides £5 to £6.50, sticky toffee pudding £8.50. Call it £35 to £40pp with a drink.
A shortcut if the morning overruns. From St Paul’s take the Central line to Holborn, change to the Piccadilly and get out at Russell Square — the station is on Bernard Street at the foot of Marchmont Street. About twenty minutes door to door against thirty on foot, and it buys back the whole difference.

Windsor Castle
📍  Windsor SL4 1NJ  ·  5 to 10 minutes on foot from either station
💲  About £30 to £33pp  ·  Book: rct.uk 🕐  10:00 to 17:15, last admission 16:00 ⏱  3 to 4 hours

Nine hundred and fifty years of continuous occupation on the same lump of chalk above the Thames, begun by William the Conqueror and still a working royal residence. It is not a ruin and it is not a museum: the flag over the Round Tower tells you whether the King is in. Go in at opening, take the free audio guide, and do not rush the state rooms.

  • The State Apartments: St George’s Hall with the ceiling of Garter knights’ arms, the Waterloo Chamber, the Grand Reception Room, and rooms hung with Rembrandt, Rubens, Van Dyck and Canaletto. Much of this was rebuilt after the 1992 fire and the restoration is close to invisible.
  • The Semi-State Rooms, October to March only: George IV’s private apartments, and the most extravagantly decorated rooms in the whole castle. Shut all summer. This is the single best reason to come in October.
  • Queen Mary’s Dolls’ House: a 1920s house built at one twelfth scale with running water, working lifts, electricity, tiny bottles of real wine in the cellar and a library of miniature books written by hand by actual authors. Quietly the most astonishing thing in the building.
  • St George’s Chapel: the Garter stalls with their banners, the fan vaulting, and the tombs — Henry VIII, Charles I, Queen Elizabeth II and the Duke of Edinburgh. Open Monday to Saturday to visitors, closed Sundays. Included in the ticket.
  • Changing the Guard, 11:00: it does not run daily, and Friday is often not a guard day. Check householddivision.org.uk in the week beforehand rather than planning the morning around it. Free to watch from outside the walls if it is on.
Bags and security: airport-style screening at the entrance and no large bags allowed inside. Travel with one small bag between you and you will walk straight through.

Recommended — Friday the 9th, the first half of Day 6 The museum is an eight-minute walk from Holborn station, which is where that whole day already starts and ends, so nothing needs a new journey. Go in at the 10am opening, out at one, and rejoin the day at Seven Dials Market with the Hunterian still at 2:30pm and the table still at 7pm. What it costs you: the first half of the morning — Neal’s Yard first thing, Cecil Court, and Maison Bertaux. That is a real loss, and Neal’s Yard in particular only works early. A partial version: Neal’s Yard at 10am as planned, into the museum at 10:45am for two hours rather than three, and pick the crawl up at Venchi. Tight, but it keeps the photograph. Watch the two collisions. Friday is one of the three days Windsor can go on — you cannot have both, though Windsor wrecks the evening anyway. More importantly, this day ends at Holborn at about half three to leave two hours for changing, and that is not negotiable. Three hours of British Museum in the morning is fine; letting it run into the afternoon is not.
🦖
Thursday the 8th — instead of the Natural History Museum, and almost certainly not A free museum morning swapped for a free museum morning, with the change, the five o’clock table and Totoro all untouched. The journey is easier too: Holborn rather than the South Kensington run. What it costs you: the blue whale, the dinosaurs and Hintze Hall — permanently. The Natural History Museum has exactly one day available in the whole fortnight: it is closed to the public on the 9th, and every other London day is spoken for. Giving Thursday to the British Museum does not postpone the natural history museum, it cancels it. The week was built around natural history over antiquities. Take this only if that preference has genuinely reversed for both of you.
✈️
Saturday the 17th — the last morning, and the flight time allows it The option that costs nothing at all. QR 012 leaves Terminal 4 at 19:25, with bags to be collected from the hotel by about 3pm, so the last morning is genuinely free. The museum is about twenty minutes on foot from the King’s Cross hotel, or five on the 59, 91 or 168 bus down Southampton Row. In at 10am, two hours on the ground floor, back for the noon check-out and leave the bags at the desk. What it costs you: only the canal towpath and Granary Square morning, which is a pleasant hour rather than a fixture — and you can have both, with breakfast on the canal at nine and the museum at ten. The one discipline is to take the short route rather than the full three hours: Rosetta Stone, Assyria, Egypt, out. If it is raining, the British Library five minutes from the hotel is the easier version of the same idea.
💰
What it does to the money Nothing. Entry is free on any of the three dates, and every day it displaces is either free itself or already inside the travel budget. The one thing to actually do is cancel any free ticket you are no longer using — the Hunterian slot on the 9th, or the Natural History Museum slot on the 8th — so someone else can have it. Nothing on the cost sheet changes either way.

If the 9th turns out to be pouring, three dry hours in Bloomsbury beats walking a sweet crawl between five doorways in it — and the Hunterian at 2:30pm and the table at 7pm both still happen exactly as written.

Why this is an appendix and not a fixture. The parks are free, need no booking, and are the only thing in the London week that can be decided on the morning itself. They fit in front of the museum on the 8th and nowhere else, because nothing else in the week has a slow enough start to absorb them.
The honest assessment: you will probably skip this, and that is fine. It only works if you are out of the hotel by half nine on the Thursday, the morning after Leavesden gets you home at a quarter to ten. Nothing downstream depends on it — the museum slot is at 10:30 and the day works identically whether you arrive from a park or from bed. Decide it when the alarm goes off.

🌳  The royal parks, free
St James’s Park, Buckingham Palace & Green Park
📍  St James’s Park, London SW1A 2BJ  ·  St James’s Park station on the District line
Free 🕐  Open from dawn, never booked ⏱  45 minutes at a stroll

The oldest of the royal parks and the prettiest: a long lake with two islands, pelicans that have been kept here since 1664, and the view from the Blue Bridge back toward Whitehall, which is the one everybody photographs and is genuinely worth it. Out at the west end for the Buckingham Palace exterior and the Victoria Memorial, then north through Green Park under the plane trees.

  • The route makes it work. St James’s Park station to South Kensington is four stops on the District line, about ten minutes, so the park empties straight into the museum with no detour and no second journey.
  • Do not wait for Horse Guards. The Changing of the King’s Life Guard is a full mounted change only on Mondays, Wednesdays and Fridays; Tuesdays and Thursdays get a smaller inspection instead. On the 8th you would be standing about until eleven for the lesser version and losing the quiet first hour of the museum for it. That trade is the wrong way round.
  • October helps. The plane trees are turning, the light is low and long, and the park is close to empty before ten.

What it costs you if you do go: the first forty-five minutes of the museum, and nothing else. You would reach South Kensington at about 10:30 rather than 10:00, which is exactly the booked slot — so in practice this is free, provided you are actually out of the door by half nine. If you are not up by nine, do not chase it — go straight to the museum, arrive for the opening, and take the quiet hour instead.
